## Obiettivo

Add to `ralmon.eval` what ADR-074 (3) and (4) require. (1) On the main population: the log score and the Brier score of `Alert.p_kappa` against the cause of the matched ground-truth event, PER TEMPLATE (equal-weight aggregate, RESP apart, as in ADR-072), with calibration curves (reliability bins with counts) per template. (2) On the confounder pairs: for each member, the log likelihood ratio between the two causes of its group, `Lambda = log P(e | c_A) - log P(e | c_B)`, computed from the per-field contributions of the alert (prior-free); in the indistinguishable stratum the distribution of `|Lambda|` and its distance from 0; in the distinguishable stratum the correct direction of each member, the pair statistic `D = Lambda_A - Lambda_B`, and McNemar between methods on the direction (b = X right and Y not, c = the reverse, exact binomial on b + c). No precision on pairs. (3) `#metrics` 0.2.0: new keys in `metrics.json` (`attribution_log_score`, `attribution_brier`, `attribution_calibration_curve`, `pair_llr`, `pair_direction_mcnemar`); a method without `p_kappa` (B1 to B5) gets `null`. The definitions are also written into the metric list of WS08-T02 and the README of WS08.

**Signatures that differ from `#metrics` 0.2.0 (to be reconciled by whoever owns the contract).**
- `attribution_log_score`, `attribution_brier`, `attribution_calibration_curve` take the LABELLED ALERTS (`Sequence[LabelledAlert]` from `label_alerts`), not a `Matching`. Reason: the one-to-one `match_events` is template-agnostic and would give an ECG fault the COUNT alert; the cause of an alert is a many-to-one relation (below). They return `None` for a method without `p_kappa`; the dictionaries are keyed by the template in upper case (`TEMPLATES`) plus `AGGREGATE`; the calibration curve returns `(mean p, observed frequency, count)` per bin with `(nan, nan, 0)` for an empty bin.
- `pair_llr(members, rule)` takes the `LikelihoodRule` (the source of the cells); the contract has `pair_llr(members)`. `PairMember` has the fields of the module (pair id, member, group, variant, stratum flag, the two specific subtypes, the common template, the evidence vector, the alert).
- `pair_direction_mcnemar(x, y)` returns `{b, c, n_discordant, p}` as in the contract (`p` the exact two-sided binomial on `b + c`).

**Judgment calls.**
1. **The cause of an alert is many-to-one, tight primary.** An alert is compatible with an event if its template is one the event can violate (C1: `template_id`; C2: `details["templates"]` of the linked injection-log entry; C3: a static table of the threat model, checked in a test against the likelihood table's catalogue) and it is decided in `[first decidable, max(first decidable, end) + loose_after_ms]`. TIGHT adds, for a C2 event only (the ADR names the C2 event; the research prototype applied the closed window to every event), the data scope of the faulty device (`details["target"]`; the table is the extractor's `_DATA_SCOPE`, compared in a test; the robot needs the template to have the robot in its scope) and a window closed at the end (allowance 0). Several classes among the compatible events: the class of the INJECTED events if exactly one, else the alert is dropped as ambiguous. Counts of what was left out are in `excluded` (`no_compatible_event`, `ambiguous`, `predicted_violation`, `cascade_effect`, `unscored_tail`). The injected C2 events whose log entry has no `templates` or `target` (the mini fixtures) are unconstrained. CONSEQUENCE (decided by gab in the fix round, B1 below: the AMBIGUOUS class): a C2 event with no window (`end_ms` None: the extra opening of a `dispenser_bounce`, the per-tick faults) is active only at its onset, so under the tight labelling an alert decided later (the COUNT alert of a bounce is decided at the next dose step) is not caused by it and the alert is left out; the research prototype kept only 3 bounce alerts for the same reason (`likelihood-attribution.md` 10.1.3). So `dispenser_bounce` is almost absent from the tight tables and present in the loose ones. Giving a point event a window of `loose_after_ms` would change the labelling that was decided; I kept the ADR's reading and flag it (test `test_a_point_c2_event_is_the_tight_cause_only_at_its_own_instant`).
2. **`loose_after_ms` is an `awaiting_human_value`** (`run_options.attribution`). The research used 24 h; that is not a value of the project and no default exists. The same allowance is used for C1 and C3 events in the TIGHT labelling (a C1 event at its first decidable instant needs some slack for the monitor's decision latency). Without it every block of a detector with a posterior is `{status: "awaiting_human_value", needs: [...]}`. The fixture smoke config carries a test value (60 000 ms).
3. **Cascade effects are folded** (left out of the scores, counted) because they carry the root's `p_kappa` and `cause_set`; `effects: include` scores them against their own compatible events. The report states which (`meta.attribution.effects`).
4. **Calibration curve**: pooled one-vs-rest pairs `(p_c, y_c)` of the three classes per template, plus the per-class curves and the top-label curve with its ECE (a tie at the maximum goes to the first of C1, C2, C3: declared, not a finding). Brier is the sum form (range 0 to 2).
5. **The hierarchical rule's kappa as a set** is scored without any detector support: the applicable subtypes per template and class come from `configs/attribution_likelihood.yaml` (structure only, no cell value; its sha256 is in `meta.attribution.sets.applicable_table`). So `ours` (hierarchical) has `attribution_sets` on the smoke run, `null` for the scores.
6. **Equal coverage** takes the posteriors from `AlertInputs.posteriors` (method name to a posterior over the applicable subtypes); the first method is the primary one for the count of true subtypes that are not applicable. A fixed set (`AlertInputs.fixed_sets`, e.g. `hier`) is compared at `alpha = 1 - its coverage on the calibration part` (exact `Fraction`, written as `"n/d"`); if it covers none or all of the calibration part the comparison is not defined and says so.
7. **Pairs.** The stratum is the per-pair flag `indistinguishable_by_construction` of the injection log (never the variant id); a pair without it is counted `unknown`. The two subtypes of the pair are the subtypes of the members' relevant injected events (`presence_spoof` is ignored as a co-event). The common template is the group's primary template if `run_options.attribution.pair_primary_templates` names one, then the order of `TEMPLATES`, the first with an alert in both members: this is the provisional matching of `pairs-allocation.md` section 5 (it did not define the window) and is reported in `pair_llr.matching`, not tuned. The pairs (not their members) are split by the seed with the experiment's fractions; the test pairs are scored, the calibration pairs calibrate the separate calibrator, the main-population calibrator on the same pair test alerts is `transfer_from_main`. McNemar's unit is the pair (both members point to their own cause). The pair quantities need the detector's `likelihood_rule` and `alert_inputs` and the policy `twins: separate`; otherwise `null` or a status block.
8. **Log score with a zero probability**: the library returns `inf` and counts it; the file writes `mean` null with a `reason` (the day and cluster intervals are not computed on a non-finite statistic). The bootstrap tables use the alerts with a positive probability for the replicate statistic.
9. **Wilson intervals** use the run's `ci_level` (the normal quantile is computed, not typed). They are over alerts and anti-conservative when a patient has several alerts: the cluster interval is the headline.
10. The runner also runs the detector on the CALIBRATION patients, only when the detector has `alert_inputs`, `loose_after_ms` and `set_alphas` are set; the counters and run details of the detector are restored (a test checks `analysis.run.detections`).
