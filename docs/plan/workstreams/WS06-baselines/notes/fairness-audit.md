# Fairness audit of the baselines B1 to B5

Task WS06-T07, analisi §10 objection 1 ("the evaluation is circular: whoever writes the specification wins"), §5.4 and §4.4. Code: `src/ralmon/baselines/audit.py`. Tests: `tests/baselines/test_fairness_audit.py` (fixtures in `tests/baselines/conftest.py`). Companions: `B5-deviations.md`, `B5-failure-analysis.md`. Technical, pre-clinical validation only: every number below that comes from a fixture is FIXTURE-ONLY (fixture registry F0, ADR-027), a measurement on a hand-built day and not a statistic.

## Scope and method

What is audited. Whether each baseline (i) receives the clinical knowledge of the proposed method that its definition allows (bands and schedules for B1, all seven templates for B3 and B5, none for B2 and B4 by design), (ii) sees the same inputs at the same time, (iii) never reads a latent trace or ground truth at inference, and (iv) is fitted, calibrated and tuned only on data that the proposed method may also use.

Method. `run_audit` fits every detector through the harness (`BaselineBase.fit`) on the same days, runs it on the same observed day and reads what it touched from the logs of `ralmon.baselines.access`: the `AccessLog` of the guarded days (split, field, refused attempts, reads allowed only by `calib_latent`), the `ReadLog` filled by `AuditedRegistry` / `LoggingRegistry` (registry names, `bounds:<signal>`) and by `logged_plan` (plan parameter names), a spy on `plan.schedule` (the anchors of the instances are not logged by name), a probe on each forecaster (`fit` and `predict` calls, the patients of the days it was fitted on), and the output of the same run with a populated `EvidenceVector` mapping and with a forged plan signature. The days are poisoned: 2 training days and 19 calibration days whose latent trace and ground truth raise `AssertionError` on any access, so a read that the log missed would still fail the run. The test day carries a violation of each of the seven templates. The plan holds the seven templates with the values of the fixture registry F0: a parameter record (`anchor_ms` None) for RANGE, HOLD, COUNT, RESP, SYS and INT, two anchored SCHED instances with their own window, and the day-end COUNT. Timing is one `TimingConfig` for all (step 60 s, reorder delay two steps, tail 25 h), equal to the `MonitorConfig` of the proposed method. Operating parameters are test values (`alpha` 0.2, `z_threshold` 3.0, `score_quantile` 0.9), none tuned.

The proposed method is audited with the same wrappers, not described from memory (but as a stand-in: `_audit_proposed` in `audit.py` reconstructs it from the monitor, the calibrator and `build_calibration_episodes`; it is not the `OursDetector` of the experiment, and WS08 must re-run the audit with the real one): its monitor runs on the same day (`run_offline_detailed` in predictive mode) with a `LoggingRegistry` and the logged plan, and its calibration episodes are built by `build_calibration_episodes` from the observed traces of guarded calibration days (`GuardedDay.observed`), with `SplitConformal` and a persistence forecaster behind a probe. Two cells of its column cannot be shown by a log and are declared by the contract (`EvidenceVector` consumed by the attribution layer; plan signature verified by `run_offline(verifier=...)`, ADR-053); they are marked `by design`.

Which calibration target the proposed method uses (the question of `CalibrationEpisode.sigma_true`, "latent or measured" in `#conformal-calibrator`). The proposed method calibrates on the OBSERVED completion. `build_calibration_episodes` (`src/ralmon/monitor/offline.py`) takes `days: Sequence[ObservedTrace]` and has no parameter that could carry a latent day; the `sigma_true` of an episode is computed by `_observed_sigma_true` (`src/ralmon/monitor/predictive.py`) with the point evaluator on the accepted observed inputs from the activation step to the closing step of the day (a late measurement counts as lateness, G-SCHED-3); the contract text of `#monitor` rule 9 says the same ("`sigma_true` is the observed completion; no latent day can be passed"). Test 10 shows it with the poisoned days and shows that the three calibration targets are one quantity: for the response template the score is the drop of the day, in mmHg for B5, in units of the response scale for the proposed method, and the largest residual magnitude of the day for B4.

Status tags. The task writes `[resolved|escalated]`; this note uses `closed` for `resolved` (verified, nothing left to do), `escalated` as the task has it (a decision of gab is needed), and adds `accepted` (a documented asymmetry with a stated direction of bias that the audit judges tolerable, with the condition under which it is). The tag `resolved` of the task text is `closed`.

What the audit certifies. Only the fixture configuration of `tests/baselines/conftest.py` (`build_audit_setup`): the detectors, plans and days that the experiment will build are not audited by it, and the audit must be re-run on them (see the Conclusion). Test 2 (`test_shared_timing_configuration`) is a check of the fixture's construction; `test_effective_timing_matches_the_configuration` checks the step, the delay and the tail that each baseline's run uses.

Reconciliations with the task text (each is also a test docstring). (1) The knowledge set K of test 5 is the union of `params`, `scale_param`, `priority_param` and `max_verdict_latency_param` of the loaded templates (the text of the task) together with the names that the monitor of the proposed method reads on the audited day, because it reads three names that are not template fields (F2). (2) Test 12 holds in the documented-difference form of the hand-over of WS06-T01: the baseline detection time is `t_eval_ms + D`. (3) Test 15 holds for B4 as written and for B5 in the form of the hand-over of WS06-T06: the predicted alerts are nested in `alpha`, the detections are not. (4) The audit adds rows and checks that the task does not list (hold look-back, day-end minimum, schedule ticks, verdict-latency bound, ground truth of training days, probes of asymmetries (a) to (c) of `base.py`, cross-midnight state, the INT blind spot).

What the audit does not prove (threat model of `access.py`, hand-over of WS06-T01). The guards are a defence against accidents plus an audit log, not a sandbox. A deliberate cheat is feasible (`guarded._day`, a `fit` override that reads `calib_days[i].latent` from the `BenchmarkDay` it is handed, `access_log.clear()`, `LoggedMapping._inner`, `import ralmon.benchmark`); the split label is trusted; the logs are only as complete as the code paths that use the wrappers (`plan.schedule` and an unwrapped registry are not logged). The audit shows what well-behaved baselines read; it does not prove that no baseline could read more. Mitigations in the tests: the poisoned days, the source scan of test 16, and the by-design claims that a log can contradict.

Reading the matrix. B1 is a minimal threshold baseline: it reads the band, the hold threshold, the schedule window and the dose limit, and not the hold look-back, the day-end minimum, the response, system and integrity parameters (F8). B3 and B5 read every template parameter and no scale, priority or verdict-latency name, and their read sets are equal (test 8). B2 and B4 read no registry name and no plan parameter at all; B2 uses `plan.schedule` for its forest features, B4 does not touch the plan. B1, B3 and B5 touch `plan.schedule` only through `evaluation_end_ms` (the end of the run); that read is incidental. Nobody reads a latent trace, nobody reads the ground truth of training days, the calibration days give the observed trace only, and the output of every baseline is independent of the evidence and of the plan signature (true by construction of the harness, which drops the evidence before `_run`: it tests the harness and cannot discriminate a baseline).

## Documented asymmetries

Each asymmetry has a direction of bias on the comparison and a verdict: tolerable (`accepted` finding) or in need of a decision of gab (`escalated` finding, with the number of the finding). Sizes of the three harness asymmetries and of the cross-midnight effect are measured on small fixtures by the probes (a control day and a perturbed day that differ in one thing; the table is the number of detections that the perturbation adds, per detector, `ours` counting violated instances of the monitor; `B5` merges the instances of one step into one detection, so its counts are lower bounds).

- duplicate deliveries: asymmetry (b): the monitor deduplicates by (source, name, seq), the harness does not
- late input: asymmetry (a): the monitor excludes a late input; the baselines drop it by the rule of the grid
- future-stamped input: asymmetry (c): the monitor rejects an input stamped in the future (with a clock-skew bound, which the fixture registry lacks: the probe supplies a test value); the harness delivers it at its time
- deadline after midnight: the window of a late dose closes after the day: the per-day monitor ends PENDING
- integrity violation without a point value: ADR-063: the point evaluator has no value for an interval whose upper bound is negative
- state across midnight (two doses the evening before): ADR-061: the trailing 24 h counter needs the day before; Detector.run has no history input; ours without the history of the probe: +0

1. No attribution and no `EvidenceVector` for the baselines (analisi §5.4: B1 shows the value of attribution, B5 fails on it). Direction: handicaps the baselines on every attribution metric, by design; the matrix shows that their output is independent of the evidence. Accepted (F16).
2. No probabilistic confidence for the baselines, and B5 has no alpha allocation (analisi §4.4): B5 tests every predicted instance at the level `alpha`, the proposed method at `alpha / n_tests`; B4 allocates over its signals only when given an allocation. Direction, per tested instance: a larger level gives a smaller constant and so MORE alerts (more recall and more false alarms) for B5 at `alpha` than at `alpha / n_tests`; only the net effect depends on the number of instances B5 tests against those the proposed method tests. WS08 runs B5 also at `BonferroniAllocation.per_test_exact(alpha, n_tests)`. Accepted with that condition (F17). The literal rule of the paper (alert unless satisfaction is certified, D4) is a different B5 and is F25.
3. Plan signature verification is not applied to the baselines: it is a security check, not clinical knowledge; the output of every baseline is independent of the signature (matrix row). Accepted (F16).
4. B1 receives the hold threshold and the dose limit as static thresholds, which is stronger than the minimal B1 of the analysis (bands and schedule), chosen on purpose; and B1 is nevertheless minimal: no HOLD look-back window, no day-end COUNT minimum (the matrix shows `partly read`), no RESP, SYS or INT rule, a constant score. Further differences from the monitor, each pinned by a test of WS06-T02: R3 credits a bare pressure sample with no MEASUREMENT event (the monitor's SCHED does not); a MEASUREMENT stamped 1 ms before the anchor counts for the monitor's grid and not for B1; R1 alarms per pressure sample while the monitor's RANGE instance is per MEASUREMENT event; an input stamped within one step before the day is step 0 for B1 and for the monitor (B3 skips it). Escalated (F8, F9).
5. B2 and B4 receive no clinical thresholds: data-driven by design (their question is how unusual a reading is, not whether it violates the plan). They see a change between two readings of one signal, never a level, an absence or an event; B2's forest is level-shift invariant (inherent to any band-free detector) and, as a joint 13-feature forest, a weak detector of one out-of-range value (reviewer's measurement: mean score 0.518 against 0.470 on normal rows, threshold 0.566; rows in alarm 5% against 1%; a forest on `last_bp` and `age_bp` alone: 76% against 0%). Direction: the baselines are blind to the classes that need a level or an event, by construction. WS08 reports them per class and subtype. Escalated for the forest variant (F14), accepted otherwise (F16).
6. All conformal methods, and B2's z-scale and forest threshold, calibrate on unlabelled data. The audit does NOT show that anomalies in calibration days inflate the quantiles of the methods equally, and they do not: B4's `day_max` episode is the largest residual magnitude of its day, so an out-of-band or injected reading on a calibration day sets the episode directly and widens B4's quantile; an episode of B5 or of the proposed method moves only if the anomaly falls inside a tested instance (a RESP or SCHED instance with a prediction). Direction: the contamination costs B4 power more directly than it costs the proposed method (the audit calibration days carry an out-of-band reading each, and B4's episodes are those readings). Whether the calibration days are anomaly-free, or how contaminated ones are treated, is a decision of gab (F19).
7. B4, B5 and the proposed method differ in what the calibration unit is: B4 `day_max` (one episode per patient, day and signal), B5 one episode per predicted instance (RESP only), the proposed method one per predicted instance (SCHED and RESP). `CalibrationEpisode.template` of B4 is a placeholder. Accepted (F4), with the decision on `unit` and the signal set escalated (F15).
8. Harness against monitor, asymmetries (a) to (c) of `base.py`. (a) Late inputs: the monitor excludes them; `iter_steps` delivers them, but every baseline (B1, B2, B3, B4, B5) drops them with the lateness rule of the grid, which equals the monitor's when the reorder delay is a multiple of the step (probe `late input`: no difference). The statement of the hand-over of WS06-T06 that B1 to B4 may use a late input is superseded: they were completed after it. (b) Duplicates: the monitor deduplicates by (source, name, seq), the harness does not; B1, B3 and B5 raise extra alarms and count a dose twice, B4 and B2 do not (a duplicate has no residual) (probe `duplicate deliveries`; the reviewer of WS06-T04 measured 1,988 B3-only detections against about 3,500 agreeing on 150 seeds). Direction: false alarms for B1, B3, B5. (c) Future stamps: the monitor rejects an input stamped beyond the clock-skew bound, the harness delivers it at its device time (probe `future-stamped input`; the fixture registry F0 has no `clock_skew_max_ms`, so with F0 the monitor does not check, and the probe supplies a test value). Direction unclear. Whether `iter_steps` should mirror (b) and (c) is open for gab (F18). The benchmark may emit none of the three: WS08 reports the counts of rejected inputs of the monitor on the test split (`run_offline_detailed(...).rejected_inputs` by reason), which sizes (a) to (c) on the real data.
9. Reorder delay that is not a multiple of the step: the lateness rule of B1, B2, B3, B4 and B5 diverges from the monitor's (the reviewer of WS06-T05: 280 of 400 trials at 30,000 ms, 362 of 400 at 120,001 ms; exact at 0 and at two steps). The audit reports such a configuration as a problem (`test_audit_flags_a_delay_that_is_not_a_multiple_of_the_step`). Escalated (F10).
10. Detection time. The grid time is a monitor time, so a baseline reports `t_eval_ms + D` (rounded up to the grid) where the monitor's record has `t_eval_ms` (test 12; for B3, B5 and B1 on the one-violation day, B2 and B4 report the delivery step of the sample). A latency comparison with the proposed method is shifted by D unless one convention is chosen. The Notes of WS06-T01 (post-task review) keep "whether latency comparisons are shifted by the reorder delay D" open for gab. Escalated (F20).
11. Detections after midnight are kept: B3, B5 and B1 run on for `tail_ms` and detect at a step of the next day (a RESP window or a SCHED deadline that closes after midnight), where the per-day monitor ends PENDING (probe `deadline after midnight`). Direction: extra false alarms against a per-day truth, for the baselines. Escalated (F11).
12. State across midnight (ADR-061): `Detector.run` has no `history` input, so the baselines run one day at a time with empty state, whereas `run_offline(history=...)` carries the 24 h counter, the held pressure and the open windows into the day (probe `state across midnight`: with the day before the monitor reports additional violated instances, without it the proposed method is as blind as the baselines). Direction: favours the proposed method if it is given history and the baselines are not. The plan is to give no history to either, or to report both. Escalated (F12).
13. B3 and B5 are silent on INT and SYS violations that the monitor reports (ADR-063; probe `integrity violation without a point value`; the reviewer's differential: SYS 16 of 927 and INT 288 of 930 monitor-VIOLATED instances silent, none for RANGE, HOLD, COUNT, SCHED, RESP). Direction: lowers B3 and B5 on C3/INT and SYS for a reason that has nothing to do with conformal prediction or normalisation. Escalated (F7).
14. B5 has a predictive part for RESP only (D2), while the proposed method also predicts SCHED with the `DeadlineForecaster`; B5 then reduces to B3 plus a rarely firing RESP test and may be called a straw man. Escalated (F6).
15. B3 and B5 INT results depend on the presence gate: while the benchmark generator emits no `NODE_STATE colocated` report (WS04-T11), `presence_samples_without_availability_marks` is positive (it is 1 on the audit day) and INT-based results are not evidence; B5 `run_stats` and `B3PlainSTL.run_stats` expose it. Accepted with that condition (F21).
16. The score of a baseline is not comparable with the proposed method's: B1 is binary (constant 1.0), B3 is minus the raw robustness in the unit of each template, B4's `|r| / q(alpha)` exists only for alarms and changes with alpha, B5's score saturates at the largest float for minus infinity, B2's scores are in units of their own thresholds.
