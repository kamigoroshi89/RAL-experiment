## Published method as read

The paper gives two algorithms, a direct one and an indirect one. B5 implements the direct one. The bullets state what the paper does, not what B5 does.

- The system is a stochastic process with unknown distribution D over discrete-time trajectories X = (X_0, X_1, ...) with X_tau in R^n, one state per time step; the paper makes no assumption on D (locator: Section 2, p. 4).
- Assumption 1: K independent realisations of D are available, and they are split into a training set (to learn the predictor) and a calibration set D_cal (locator: Assumption 1, p. 4; Section 2.2, p. 5).
- The specification is a bounded STL formula phi, enabled at a time tau_0; its formula length L_phi gives the number of future states needed to decide it (locator: Assumption 2 and the definition of L_phi, p. 5 and Appendix A, p. 24).
- The predictor is any measurable map from the observed prefix (x_0, ..., x_t) to the predicted states (x_hat_{t+1|t}, ..., x_hat_{t+H|t}); it is learned from the training set and is independent of the specification (locator: Section 2.2, p. 5; Section 3.2, p. 8, last paragraph before Remark 1).
- Problem 1: given the prefix x_obs observed at the current time t, decide whether P((X, tau_0) satisfies phi) >= 1 - delta holds; the probabilities are unconditional (locator: Section 2.3, Problem 1, p. 6; footnote 1, p. 4).
- The direct method needs the horizon H = tau_0 + L_phi - t and builds the predicted trajectory x_hat = (x_obs, x_hat_{t+1|t}, ..., x_hat_{t+H|t}) (locator: Section 3.2, equation (2), p. 7).
- Nonconformity score of one calibration trajectory: R(i) = rho_phi(x_hat(i), tau_0) - rho_phi(x(i), tau_0), the predicted robust semantics minus the true one; a positive score means the prediction was too optimistic (locator: Section 3.2, p. 8, definition of R(i) and the sentence after it).
- Region constant: C = R_(p), the p-th smallest score with p = ceil((|D_cal| + 1)(1 - delta)), where the score |D_cal| + 1 is set to infinity (locator: Theorem 1, equation (4), p. 8; Section 3.1, p. 7).
- Decision rule and guarantee: if rho_phi(x_hat, tau_0) > C then P((X, tau_0) satisfies phi) >= 1 - delta. The rule only certifies satisfaction; the paper states nothing for the opposite case (locator: Theorem 1, p. 8; equation (3), p. 7).
- The guarantee is marginal: the probability is over the test trajectory and the calibration set, not conditional on D_cal; the proof uses independent and identically distributed scores (locator: Remark 2, p. 8; proof of Theorem 1, p. 8).
- The prediction time t is fixed a priori and the calibration set is fixed a priori for that t; the authors note that strictly one would resample a calibration set for each test trajectory and treat the fixed set as acceptable when it is large (locator: Section 4, opening paragraph, p. 11).
- Special case tau_0 = t: the specification is enabled at the current time, which is the online use (locator: Section 2.3, p. 6, last paragraph).
- A conjunction of formulas has robustness equal to the minimum of the conjuncts, and the negation has robustness minus the robustness of the argument (locator: Appendix A, robust semantics, p. 24).
- Indirect method: prediction regions C_tau for every future state with the level delta divided by H, a worst-case robust semantics over those regions, and a formula in positive normal form (locator: Section 3.3, Lemma 1 and equation (6), p. 9; Theorem 2, p. 10). The authors state that it is more conservative and needs a larger calibration set, and that it can point at the predicates that may be violated (locator: Remark 4, p. 10).
- Evidence in the paper: one aircraft case with 5680 calibration trajectories, dense state sampling at 100 Hz, one specification, delta = 0.05; one driving case with 200 calibration trajectories where only the direct method is run because calibration data are limited (locator: Section 4.1, p. 11; Section 4.2, pp. 13 to 15, footnote 7 on p. 15).
- The paper has no notion of several specifications with different units, of a per-instance severity scale, of an alert with an attributed cause, or of an allocation of delta over several tests per trajectory (locator: whole paper, absence checked on Sections 1 to 5; the only attribution-like statement is the worst-case predicate analysis of the indirect method, Remark 4, p. 10).

## Mapping to the pathway templates

1. **One trajectory is one observed patient-day** on the monitor grid of step `T_s`, built from the harness batches with the signal construction of `ralmon.signals` (held signals `bp`, `cuff_health`, `inflation_ms`, `presence`; impulse counts `meas`, `dispense`, `intake`, `sched_tick`, `auth`; derived `dose_count`, `bp_pre`, `bp_drop`, `meas_count`). A day starts with empty carry-over (ADR-061, proposed), as the other baselines.
2. **One instance of a template is one specification phi_i** with `tau_0` equal to its anchor: RANGE and SYS at every MEASUREMENT event, HOLD, COUNT (dose limit), RESP and INT at every DISPENSE event, SCHED at every tick of the plan, COUNT (day-end obligation) at the end of the day. The instances are created exactly as the monitor creates them. The value `rho_phi_i` is the raw robustness of the point evaluator of `ralmon.monitor` (`make_point_evaluator`), in the unit of the template, never scaled and never clipped.
3. **Decided at the anchor means H = 0.** RANGE, SYS, HOLD, COUNT (limit) and INT have a closed decision window at the anchor step, so `H = tau_0 + L_phi - t = 0` and x_hat equals the observed trajectory (equation (2), p. 7): the constant is C = 0 and the rule reduces to the sign of the measured robustness. B5 therefore has no predictive part for them. This is not a choice.
4. **Instances with a future part.** RESP (window `delta2` after the dose, signal `bp`) is the only template whose open part is a continuous signal that the `Forecaster` contract can predict. SCHED needs the arrival of a measurement (an event), COUNT day-end needs future dispenses (events through a counter): the trajectory model of the paper has no event predictor, so they get no predictive part (deviation D2). For RESP the prediction instant is the activation step of the instance, the prefix is the delivered samples up to then, the forecast path of `bp` replaces the unknown future `bp`, and the unknown future measurements are placed at the measurement ticks of the plan (the anchors of its SCHED instances; deviation D3). The completed trace is evaluated by the same point evaluator and gives `rho_hat`.
5. **True robustness.** `rho_true` of the same instance is the raw robustness of the evaluator on the complete observed day (the observed trace; the latent trace is never read).
6. **Calibration.** Direct method (Theorem 1) applied to the negated specification, because the alert is about violation (deviation D4): `R = rho_true - rho_hat`, the calibrator of `ralmon.conformal` receives `sigma_hat = rho_hat` and `sigma_true = rho_true` in raw units, `C` is its `quantile(alpha)` of the one-sided score, and the alert is emitted iff `rho_hat + C < 0`. Variant `B5` (aggregation `"conjunction"`): one calibrator, one score per prediction step, the robustness of the step is the minimum of the raw robustness of the instances evaluated at that step (the conjunction rule of Appendix A, p. 24). Variant `B5-per-template` (aggregation `"per_template"`): one calibrator per template, one decision per instance.
7. **Decision after the deadline.** Once an instance is decided the decision is the sign of its measured raw robustness (strictly negative is a violation, as `VIOLATED iff hi < 0` in `#verdict`), which is equation (2) with no future part left. A detection is emitted on each rising edge of the decision of an instance, so an instance that was alerted by the prediction and confirmed by the measurement gives one detection, at the earlier time.

| Template | Unit | Open signal at the anchor | Predictive part in B5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| RANGE | mmHg | none (decided at the anchor) | no, H = 0 |
| SYS | ms | none | no, H = 0 |
| HOLD | mmHg | none | no, H = 0 |
| COUNT (limit) | dose | none | no, H = 0 |
| INT | ms | none | no, H = 0 |
| COUNT (day end) | dose | future dispenses (events, counter) | no, no event predictor (D2) |
| SCHED | ms | arrival of a measurement (event) | no, no event predictor (D2) |
| RESP | mmHg | future blood pressure (continuous, sparse) | yes |

## Deviations

| Id | Aspect | Paper | This implementation | Reason | Effect on B5 strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Sampling of the trajectory | A state at every time step, 100 Hz in the aircraft case (Section 4.1, p. 11) | Blood pressure arrives a few times a day; the grid value is the held last measurement and the forecaster extrapolates it over hours | The data of this problem; nothing else is available | Weakens, and it is the sparse-sampling failure mode that the analysis must show |
| D2 | Events, counters, ticks | Real-valued states with a trajectory predictor (Section 2.2, p. 5) | SCHED, COUNT (day end) and INT get no predictive part: before the deadline they produce nothing, after it the measured sign | The task text (test 15). The paper has no event predictor. The proposed method predicts SCHED with `DeadlineForecaster` (WS05-T05, ADR-060); B5 does not | Weakens B5 against the proposed method; it is the task's reading of the published method. Open question recorded in the task Notes |
| D3 | Completion of what the forecaster cannot predict | The predictor outputs every state (Section 3.2, p. 7) | For RESP: measurements at the measurement ticks of the plan (SCHED anchors), no other future event, counters and held status signals kept at their last value, `bp` from the forecast | The plan is shared clinical knowledge, the same as the monitor uses (`Predictor._complete`) | Neutral, equal to the proposed method |
| D4 | Direction of the guarantee | Certifies satisfaction: alarm-free when `rho_hat > C` with `R = rho_hat - rho_true` (Theorem 1, p. 8) | Theorem 1 applied to the negated formula (`rho` of the negation is minus `rho`, p. 24): `R = rho_true - rho_hat`, alert iff `rho_hat + C < 0`, error event "alert emitted and `rho_true >= 0`" bounded by `alpha` | `#conformal-calibrator` rules 3 and 6 and Proposition 1 use this form; the direct method needs no positive normal form (p. 9). The literal rule "alert unless satisfaction is certified" has a different error event (missed violations) and alerts whenever the region is wide | Neutral for the guarantee. The literal rule would give higher recall and many more false alarms; not run, a variant for WS08 if the human wants it |
| D5 | Several formulas | One formula per trajectory; a conjunction has the minimum robustness (Appendix A, p. 24) | `B5`: minimum of the raw robustness of the instances evaluated at the same step, one pooled calibrator; `B5-per-template`: one calibrator per template, one decision per instance | The paper is silent on instances anchored at different times; the minimum rule is its only multi-formula statement, hence the primary variant; the per-template variant is the strongest fair setting | `B5` can be masked by the unit mixing (mixed-units failure); `B5-per-template` cannot at the level of the decision |
| D6 | Calibration unit | A trajectory (Assumption 1, p. 4) | One prediction episode of one instance of one patient-day (conjunction: one prediction step); the split by patient is done by the caller, B5 refuses nothing | `#conformal-calibrator` rule 1; days of one patient are not independent, which the harness does not check | Neutral; coverage is marginal over patient episodes under exchangeability |

| D8 | Horizon | `H = tau_0 + L_phi - t` (Section 3.2, p. 7) | The forecast covers the rest of the day; the deadline of the instance is the step at which the completed trace first gives a value; an instance that does not close before the end of the day gets no prediction and is counted | `L_phi` would have to be read from the registry (`max_verdict_latency` entry), which B3 does not read; the path beyond the deadline is not used | Neutral for the value of `rho_hat`; the longer forecast only costs time |
| D9 | Confidence parameter | `delta`, fixed a priori; Remark 1 suggests a grid search over `delta` (p. 8) | `alpha` equals `delta`, no default, supplied by the experiment config; the grid is swept by WS08 as for the other methods and nothing is chosen on test days | Rule 5 of the WS06 README | Neutral |
| D10 | Several tests per patient-day | A single `delta` for a single specification (Theorem 1, p. 8) | No allocation: the per-test level is `alpha`. With `m` tested instances in a day the probability of at least one false predictive alert can be up to `m alpha` | The paper has none; the proposed method has the union bound (`BonferroniAllocation`) | Raises recall and false alarms of B5 against the proposed method; documented, not hidden |
| D11 | Target trace of the true robustness | The true trajectory x(i) (Section 3.2, p. 8) | The observed completed day: noise and faults of the observed trace stay in the score; the latent trace is never read (`AccessPolicy.calib_latent` stays False) | `#detector`: no detector sees `LatentDay`; the paper's states are observed exactly, ours are not | Neutral to slightly weakening: contamination by injected faults widens the quantile, equally for every conformal method |
| D12 | Units and scale | Single specification, no scale (Section 4) | Raw robustness in the unit of each template, no clinical scaling, no clipping, no priority | Definition of B5 (analisi §5.4); the mixed-units failure | Weakens by design |
| D13 | Infinite robustness | Real-valued | A completed trace with a robustness of minus or plus infinity (an indicator atom fails) makes no prediction and no calibration episode and is counted; the measured decision at the deadline still applies | The calibrator refuses non-finite values; the monitor avoids the case by clipping, which B5 must not do | Slightly weakens: certain violations of the completed trace are not predicted |
