# Diagnosability matrix, declared limits, hazards and standards

Document WS02-T05. It renders the machine-readable matrix `configs/diagnosability.yaml` (contract `02-interfaces.md#attribution`, version 0.1.0, not edited here), states its declared limits, and adds the hazards and standards paragraph and the hazard register that close R3 weaknesses 4 and 5 for the paper. The matrix keeps the ten rows of analisi §4.5 (`DX-01` to `DX-10`) with the two rows that are not distinguishable (`DX-04`, silent cuff bias; `DX-10`, informed attacker coherent on every channel) as declared results and not as margin notes (analisi §10, fourth objection), and adds rows (`basis: inferred`) for every subtype of the fault and threat vocabularies and for the WS04 subtypes of class C1. Every row carries an outcome with the embodied channel removed (ablation A4), so that WS08-T04 can report per row what the robot contributes (AE-5).

Validation in this project is technical and pre-clinical (ADR-001): nothing here is a clinical claim, no entry says that a rule, an action or a mitigation is clinically validated, and no entry carries a rate, a clinical value or a device value. Rates at which the rows occur are measured on the benchmark by WS08. The expected values of the outcomes are those of the worked cases W-01 to W-15 of `docs/models/attribution-rule.md`, or are derived from its decision table and its transition table under A4 (the row note says which). The glossary of `docs/plan/01-conventions.md` section 7 is binding: an **alert** is the output tuple, a **violation** is a violated sub-formula, the **attributed cause** is a value of C1, C2, C3 or UNDETERMINED, C3 means integrity violation (ADR-002), and DISPENSE (the dispenser opened) is not INTAKE (verified by the robot witness, ADR-004).

## Diagnosability matrix

One table row per YAML row. The columns are the row id, the event, the true class, the row verdict (`distinguishable` column, four values defined in the section Reading the matrix), the discriminating evidence (canonical `group.field` paths of the twenty fields of the `EvidenceVector`, or fields proposed in slash notation that are not in the contract), and the conditions, which list the outcomes of the row: each outcome gives the channels it assumes (`default` is the default deployment; `A4` is the embodied channel removed, `CH-WIT` and `CH-SYS-ROBOT` unavailable), the result, the expected attributed cause (several values: the result depends on residual evidence, on the template that is violated, or on whether a violation is raised; NO_ALERT: no alert is produced) and, for a partial result, the candidates that the table cannot tell apart, or, for a not distinguishable result, the class the event masquerades as. The default deployment is the unauthenticated deployment of the threat model; `CH-CRYPTO available` describes the authenticated one. Channels are those of `docs/models/evidence-channels.md`; `CH-DISP` is a proposed channel (ADR-032) that feeds no field of contract 0.1.0.

Rows with an embodied dependency, read from the pair of outcomes default and A4 (they are derived by the rule, not measured; WS08-T04 compares them with the measured effect).

- **A correct attribution is lost under A4** (the class becomes UNDETERMINED, reason insufficient): `DX-02` (drug non-response, the witness or the authentication backing and the robot task outcomes are needed), `DX-05` (robot late), `DX-15` (a kill or an uncommanded transition of the robot node, visible only through the robot component of the aggregates; a device-target fault stays C2), `DX-16` (battery depleted), `DX-17` (manipulation failure) and `DX-20` (hypotension below the hold threshold, whose exclusion set includes the robot task outcomes).
- **Only a confident C1 is replaced by an abstention** (the event is not detected better or worse, the misattribution disappears): `DX-04`, `DX-10`, `DX-11`, `DX-25` and `DX-27` on `hold` and `resp`, `DX-09` on `hold`, `DX-19`, whose templates `sched` and `hold` both read the robot task outcomes (its A4 outcome has no C1 left), and `DX-24`, where the embodied channel is the attack surface of the row.
- **No change under A4**: `DX-01`, `DX-03`, `DX-06`, `DX-07`, `DX-08`, `DX-12`, `DX-13`, `DX-14`, `DX-18`, `DX-21`, `DX-22`, `DX-23` and `DX-26`, whose evidence is ward-side or already unavailable. In particular no C3 outcome changes under A4: the rule does not read the witness pair of a violated `int` as integrity evidence (AD-3), so the hypothesis of analisi §5.6 that attribution collapses on forged dispenses without the embodied channel is not produced by this rule (worked case W-09-A4); WS08-T04 reports the measured effect and the rows where it disagrees with this column.

## Declared limits

The rows that are not distinguishable (`DX-04`, `DX-10`, `DX-11`, `DX-19`, `DX-24`, `DX-25`, `DX-26`, `DX-27`) are results of the matrix, kept in the file with `declared_limit: true`, and not defects of the fault and threat catalogues. They turn two claims that were not verifiable into declared limits (analisi §4.5).

- **`DX-04`, silent cuff bias or drift (and `DX-11`, stuck-at).** There is no independent channel on the pressure and the pulse rate stays coherent, so the vector equals that of a clinical deviation and the table gives C1 (worked case W-04). The claim "a faulty sensor cannot masquerade as a clinical event" is false for these rows. The supportable claim is the reformulation of analisi §4.8: "faults that manifest in node health, device error codes, or signal quality are attributed to C2; silent faults producing plausible values remain indistinguishable from clinical deviations". The rate at which silent faults occur and are read as clinical deviations is measured on the benchmark by WS08; no number is written here.
- **`DX-10`, plausible injection from an informed attacker who is coherent on every channel.** It is undetectable by construction: no field of the twenty separates the injected series from a clinical day, so the table gives C1 when the injected values violate a template and no alert when they satisfy all templates (worked case W-10). In the second case WS08 counts an undetected C3 event in the detection metric, not an attribution error (threat-model.md, Attacks that raise no violation). The statement is made for an informed attacker in the relay position; an attacker without keys cannot be coherent on `crypto_temporal.authenticated` in an authenticated deployment, where the keyless BLE form is C3 (worked case W-10-A): that is a different attacker and not an exception to the limit. No claim of general adversarial detection is made (ADR-002). The rate at which this row occurs is measured by WS08.
- **`DX-19` and `DX-26`, clock skew and timestamp manipulation (gap G-6).** No field carries clock health. A displacement inside the acceptance bound raises nothing and can change a verdict; beyond the bound the only evidence is `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`, which mixes late, future-stamped and replay-related inputs and is never decisive alone. A C2 cause and a C3 cause give the same Events (threat-model.md, TX-8). The field is not split and no clock-health field is proposed.
- **`DX-24` and `DX-25`, spoofed presence and physical release at the dispenser.** The robot reports what its sensors were made to perceive and no field says how the observation was obtained; the release is a genuine device output and an authenticated event proves the origin device, not the intake and not the actor (ADR-004). Only a co-located robot that checks identity can contradict them, and an informed spoof removes even that.

Further limits that are not rows of their own (each is stated in the row note and in the hazard register).

- **`DX-27`, duplicate instances of a dispenser bounce.** The fault raises no field, so a duplicate violation of `hold` or `resp` opened by the extra DISPENSE reads as C1 when the other channels are coherent (derived from the decision table, not a worked case): a C2 bounce masquerades as a clinical deviation. A duplicate `int` instance gives UNDETERMINED (hazard H-10). The dispenser evidence of ADR-032 does not change this, because no rule row reads it for these templates. The `count` instance of the same fault is `DX-07`, which is conditional on `CH-DISP`.
- **Partial rows abstain.** In `DX-09`, `DX-14`, `DX-18`, `DX-21` and `DX-22` the table can only return UNDETERMINED with a candidate set, for part of the cases; the abstention is the result and not an error.
- **C1 depends on the profile.** C1 is claimed by exclusion and needs the channels of the exclusion set to be available and denied; in the reference profile S6 of `evidence-channels.md` (no diagnostics reports, no declared deadline, no independent clocks) it is unreachable for every template, and a refusal leaves the cuff silent whatever the profile declares (attribution-rule.md, C1 exclusion conditions; OP-AR-1; ADR-044 proposed). This is a limit produced by what the profile declares and not by the clinical events.
- **Rows that need a field outside contract 0.1.0.** `DX-06`, `DX-07` and `DX-22` depend on the dispenser content and interval (gap G-1, ADR-032 proposed): with the contract as it is the table abstains, and the analisi verdict is carried by the outcome that names the proposed channel `CH-DISP`, which the rule does not reproduce.
- **Attacks that raise no violation** produce no alert (NO_ALERT) and are counted by WS08 as undetected events, not as attribution errors.

## Reading the matrix

**Row verdict.** The field `distinguishable` of a row takes four values.

One rule, applied to every row and checked by script. Let N be the outcomes of the row other than those that remove the embodied pair (the A4 outcomes are reported separately in the dependency lists above).

- `not_distinguishable`: at least one outcome that removes no channel is not distinguishable, that is it attributes the event to the class it masquerades as, with no channel able to contradict it. This is a declared limit and `declared_limit` is true exactly for these rows.
- Otherwise `conditional`: the results over N include both `distinguishable` and `partial`, so the verdict depends on a channel or capability whose loss, or whose presence, changes the result (a witness or authentication for `DX-02`, authentication for `DX-08`, the dispenser content for `DX-06` and `DX-07`, signal quality for `DX-12` and `DX-23`, the ward system channel for `DX-01` and `DX-13`).
- Otherwise `distinguishable`: every result over N is `distinguishable` (the true class may still be lost under A4, as the outcomes say).
- Otherwise `partial`: no result over N is distinguishable; even with the channels the table can only abstain with a candidate set, for part of the cases. `partial` means abstention (UNDETERMINED) with a candidate set, not an error: UNDETERMINED is a valid, scored output (ADR-010), and the candidate set names the classes that the evidence cannot tell apart.

A row is judged on its listed outcomes: the absence of an outcome for a lost channel means that the loss was not derived, not that the row is robust. The loss of a required ward-side channel makes any C1 or C2 row abstain (invariant I4a of `attribution-rule.md`).

**Outcomes.** An outcome is a set of channel assumptions (`when`), a result (`distinguishable`, `partial`, `not_distinguishable`), the expected attributed causes and the candidates or the masquerade class. The empty `when` is the default deployment: every channel available, message authentication not deployed. An unavailable channel means that all of its fields are `None` (never a negative reading); the A4 outcome is `CH-WIT` and `CH-SYS-ROBOT` unavailable, as in the A4 mask of `evidence-channels.md`; the channel assumptions that name the authentication are written with `CH-CRYPTO available`. `CH-CRYPTO` has two roles. Its sequence and time fields (`crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay`, `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`) belong to every exclusion set and are read in every deployment, so the empty `when` has them available. Its authentication field (`crypto_temporal.authenticated`) exists only where message authentication is deployed, and `CH-CRYPTO available` in a `when` selects that authenticated deployment. `CH-CRYPTO unavailable` would mean that all three fields are `None` and no row uses it. A row not affected by the embodied channel repeats the default result under A4.

**Where the values come from.** The expected causes of the default and A4 outcomes are those of the worked cases of `attribution-rule.md` (the row note names the case); where the table of that document differs from the verdict of analisi §4.5 (`DX-06`, `DX-07`) the difference is stated in the note and the analisi verdict is kept as a separate outcome that names the proposed channel. The matrix is a statement of what the rule can distinguish by design, derived from the decision table; it is not a measurement. How often each row occurs and how often the rule is right is reported by WS08 on the benchmark, with the weakness of a semi-synthetic benchmark stated.

**Using the file.** Fixtures built from the rows should read the schema and not re-type it. An outcome whose `when` names a proposed channel (`CH-DISP`) is the verdict of analisi §4.5 and cannot be reproduced by the rule until ADR-032 is accepted and a row for `count` is defined. An expected cause `NO_ALERT` cannot be produced by the attribution rule, which runs only on violated records; it is a statement about the detection. The worked cases give vectors; the rows give the expected causes and the evidence paths, and the row note names the worked case to build the vector from.

## Hazards and standards paragraph

Draft for WS12-T04 and WS12-T06.

The hazard analysis of the robot-assisted monitor is framed by three standards that we name and do not claim to meet: ISO 13482 (safety requirements for personal care robots), ISO 14971 (risk management for medical devices) and IEC 62304 (the software life cycle of medical devices). The validation reported here is technical and pre-clinical, so no conformity with any of them is claimed (ADR-001). The robot adds hazards that a fixed monitor does not have: collisions with people and obstacles, infection control for a platform that moves between rooms, and the privacy of RGB-D images in patient rooms, which the architecture limits by passing witness outcomes, not images, to the monitor. Robot presence is not patient identity, so presence, identity check and observed intake are separate readings and presence alone never backs a clinical attribution. Safety actions are hazards too: blocking a dispenser or interrupting a procedure can cause a missed dose. Clinical actions therefore stay with the clinician, who confirms them, and the autonomy of the robot is limited to stop, retry and call. If the robot is unavailable, the ward node, which supervises it by heartbeat, escalates to the nurse (ADR-003).

## Hazard register

Mitigations cite only what the sources and the accepted decisions fix: limited robot autonomy (stop, retry, call), heartbeat supervision and fallback to the nurse (ADR-003), separate witness fields, witness outcomes instead of images on `/ralmon/robot_evidence`, the signed care plan (proposed, ADR-051), and abstention (UNDETERMINED). Every entry carries an explicit status in the residual column (open, partly mitigated with the residual open, mitigated with the cost stated, or accepted as a declared limit); no entry is closed without a residual. Whatever is not fixed by them is written "open: lab safety review (WS10-T01) or clinician (WS00-T07)". No procedure, dose or clinical parameter is invented, and no residual risk is rated: the register lists what is mitigated by the architecture and what is not. The action policy it reuses is the WS02 proposal of `attribution-rule.md` (section Action policy), which is for clinician review and is not a clinically validated policy. The basis column says whether the hazard is named by a source (`source`) or derived by WS02 from the documents of the workstream (`inferred`).
