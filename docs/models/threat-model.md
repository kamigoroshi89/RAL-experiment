# Threat model for C3: integrity or plausibility violations against declared attack classes

Document WS02-T02. It is the explicit threat model behind the attributed cause C3 (ADR-002): the attacker capabilities and the two knowledge levels, the attack classes that the benchmark injects on the latent-to-observed path (WS04-T08) with the change each makes to the observed trace and the `EvidenceVector` fields it can raise, the exclusions and the non-claims. It closes R2 weakness 7 (tampering treated as "low confidence", with no adversary model) and the "fault and attack model" request of the meta-review. It describes capabilities, classes, effects on the trace and the evidence a class can or cannot leave. It contains no procedure, tool, protocol detail or payload format for acting on a real device, and no claim of real-world security assurance: validation in this project is technical and pre-clinical, and nothing here is a clinical claim.

The semantics used is the merged formal core: signals, grid, two clocks, reorder buffer, late-sample, future-stamp and duplicate policy of `docs/formal/01-signals-sampling.md` (T01 below), the templates, instance semantics and robot obligations of `docs/formal/02-templates.md` (T02), and the verdict states and markers of `docs/formal/05-three-valued-semantics.md` (T05). The C2 side of the boundary is `docs/models/fault-model.md`. Contracts are those of `docs/plan/02-interfaces.md`; none is changed here, and what the model needs and the contract lacks is recorded as a gap `TM-GAP-*` in the section Traceability.

**Reading conventions.**

1. Times are integer milliseconds since the start of the patient-day (ADR-017). A label `hh:mm` converts as `t_ms = (60 hh + mm) * 60000`; in examples the grid step is one minute (`T_s` of fixture registry F0) and no unit is written next to a fixture number.
2. FIXTURE-ONLY marks a value chosen for hand arithmetic (fixture registry F0); it has no clinical meaning. "illustrative, unverified" marks a value taken from analisi (ADR-020). Golden cases of `docs/formal/golden/` are written `G-...` (templates) and `V-...` (verdicts). The worked checks of this document are `TX-n` (section Attack classes, subsection Worked checks); each was computed by hand and re-computed by an integer-millisecond evaluator written for this task and first validated on the golden cases (not committed; its outputs are pasted in the notes of the task file). Several checks derive a statement from the definitions of two signatures or of the schema (they say so); they do not test an independent implementation.
3. Hypotheses are named `H-...` and listed once in the section Attacker capabilities. Every statement of the form "class A leaves evidence E" or "class A leaves no evidence" names the hypotheses it uses and the check that reproduces it.
4. The reading of the evidence fields used here is provisional (table in the section Attacker capabilities): the contract gives a type and a name for each field, and WS02-T03 fixes the meaning. Nothing below depends on a value of a registry parameter; parameters are names only.
5. Two deployments are distinguished, per device (whether a device authenticates its messages is a capability of the device): the unauthenticated deployment U (the device does not authenticate; `crypto_temporal.authenticated` is `None`) and the authenticated deployment A (the device authenticates). A claim that does not name a deployment holds for both.
6. Glossary of `docs/plan/01-conventions.md` section 7: a violation is a violated sub-formula and names its template; the attributed cause κ (C1, C2, C3, UNDETERMINED) is a value of the alert, not a violation, so a class name is never used as the name of a violation; a violated INT instance is "a violation of φ_INT" (the template of provenance), and C3 is a cause class (ADR-002). DISPENSE (the dispenser opened) is not INTAKE (verified only by the robot witness, ADR-004). The latent trace (noise-free, fault-free, attack-free) is used only for ground truth; attacks are applied on the path from the latent trace to the observed trace, and nothing at inference reads `LatentDay` or `GroundTruthEvent`.
7. No reference is cited from memory. Where a source would be needed the marker `[ref-needed: WS03]` is written and the analisi section that names the work is given.

## Scope and claim

**The claim (ADR-002).** Class C3 is renamed from "adversarial tampering" and is claimed only as integrity or plausibility violations against the attack classes listed in this document. It is not claimed as the detection of tampering in general, nor as general adversarial detection. The attack classes are those of the section Subtype vocabulary; they come from the three capabilities of analisi §4.5 (BLE injection and replay without keys, physical access to the dispenser, perception spoofing) and the injections of analisi §5.1 (3). Compromised robot, compromised RSM and compromised specification are out of scope (section Exclusions).

**What the two words mean here.** An integrity violation (a term of ADR-002, not a violated sub-formula) is evidence, in the `crypto_temporal` group or in the provenance of a dispense, that an input is not backed by an authentic origin (a failed verification, a repeated or non-continuing sequence number, or, with the robot co-located, a dispense with neither authentication nor witness, that is a violation of φ_INT, which is context and not an attribution; with the robot away the instance is PENDING). A plausibility violation is evidence that the trace contradicts itself (cuff pulse rate against ECG heart rate, a late or future-stamped input). Neither is by itself evidence of an attacker: each field also has benign and fault causes (section The C1, C2 and C3 boundary), and the document says where a symptom has no separating field.

**Boundary by cause, not by symptom.** An attack is an action by an actor who holds a capability of this document. A fault is a failure of a unit with no adversary (`fault-model.md`): a device fault is not a violation of the care plan, and an attack is not a fault. A patient's own behaviour that departs from the care plan is C1. Where the observed trace is the same for two causes, no field computed from the trace separates them, and this document states it as a result instead of choosing one.

**What the benchmark does.** A true C3 event is an injected event (analisi §5.1): it is in the ground truth whether or not any channel can detect it, so the undetectable cases stay in the evaluation and are reported as results (analisi §10, objection 4). The monitor and every baseline see only the observed trace.

## Assets and trust boundaries

| asset | in the trace schema | who can reach it (capability) | trust status |
|---|---|---|---|
| BLE links from the devices (cuff, ECG device, dispenser, wearable) to the ward node | `Sample` and `Event` with `t_device_ms`, `t_rx_ms`, `seq` and, where the device authenticates, a verification outcome that the schema does not carry (TM-GAP-AUTH) | BLE injection and replay without keys | untrusted channel; whether a device authenticates is a per-device capability |
| the dispenser as a physical asset | `Event` kind DISPENSE, `Sample` `auth` where deployed | physical access to the dispenser | the device reports its own state faithfully; the actor who caused the event is not identified (ADR-004) |
| robot perception (RGB-D) and its witness output | `Sample` `presence`, `Event` kind INTAKE, the `witness` fields | perception spoofing (a stimulus in the physical world) | the robot software and its outputs are trusted; what its sensors perceive is not |
| timestamps and sequence numbers | `t_device_ms`, `t_rx_ms`, `seq` of every message | BLE injection and replay (the metadata the attacker writes or rewrites) | the attack surface of time (analisi §4.9 item 2; T01 section 4.5) |
| care-plan distribution from the back-end (off the safety-critical path) to the RSM | `CarePlan` with `signature` and `plan_version`; topic `/ralmon/care_plan` | none (excluded) | trusted by assumption; the signature is checked at `Monitor.load` if one is present (`CarePlan.signature` may be `None`, and then nothing is checked) and is owned by WS05 |
| trusted computing base (TCB): the robot, the RSM (ward node: gateway, monitor, attribution) and the specification (templates, registry, care plan) | verdicts, evidence, alerts | none by assumption | outside the attacker's reach; a compromise of any of the three is excluded |

The ward node is the verifier. It receives what the gateway delivers, decides acceptance by the two clocks and the sequence numbers (T01 sections 3.4 and 4.2 to 4.4), and builds the evidence from the observed trace and from system health sources (liveliness, QoS, diagnostics, link metric). What the attacker controls is therefore the content and the metadata of what reaches the gateway, the physical actuation of the dispenser, and the physical scene in front of the robot.

## Attacker capabilities

| capability | what the attacker can do | what the attacker cannot do | preconditions | trace changes | basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLE injection and replay without keys (analisi §4.5) | deliver to the gateway messages that claim to come from a device on a BLE link: messages with chosen values (injection) or earlier genuine messages captured and delivered again (replay); write the device timestamp and the sequence number of such a message, as far as the knowledge level allows; in the benchmark, suppress the genuine messages of the targeted stream in the window (H-SUPP; an inferred sub-capability, see below) | produce a valid authentication tag where the device authenticates (no keys); change the gateway, the RSM, the care plan or the robot; make a genuine device emit a message it did not produce | within radio range of the link; for replay, a previous passive capture of the genuine messages; for the informed level, the knowledge of the section Knowledge levels | forged or re-delivered `Sample` and `Event` of the targeted device with attacker-chosen values, `t_device_ms` and `seq`; the genuine messages of the window absent (H-SUPP) | source |
| physical access to the dispenser (analisi §4.5) | actuate the dispenser (release or open it) or interfere with its sensing, so that the dispenser node reports a DISPENSE that neither the care plan nor the patient caused; the event is a genuine output of the device (its `seq`, its timestamps and, where deployed, its authentication are valid) | make the patient take the dose; produce INTAKE or any witness evidence; forge a message that passes verification for an event the device did not itself record (that needs the keys where the device authenticates) | physical proximity to the dispenser and no one to prevent it | an extra DISPENSE `Event` (and an `auth` `Sample` where the device authenticates), no INTAKE; other interference (hiding an opening, removing contents) has the footprint of the C2 device faults of `fault-model.md` | source |
| perception spoofing (analisi §4.5) | present to the robot's perception a stimulus that makes the robot report the presence of the patient (and, at the informed level, an intake) while the robot is co-located; the robot software is unmodified and its output is genuine for what it perceived | change the robot's software, its output after perception or its availability signal; make a witness appear when the robot is not co-located (the channel is then `None`, `#signals` rule); hold the robot's keys | the robot is co-located with the channel available; the attacker is in its sensing range; for the informed level, the knowledge of the robot perception model | a `presence` `Sample` of value 1.0 (and optionally an INTAKE `Event`) from the robot that the latent trace does not contain | source |

**Assumption on keys.** Attackers hold no keys: no device authentication key and no key of the care-plan signature. Physical access to the dispenser is assumed not to give access to key material (extraction is excluded); "physical access defeats authentication" below means that the genuine device authenticates an event that the actor caused, not that a key is extracted. An attacker who holds a key is the compromised-device case and is excluded (section Exclusions). Authentication separates a forged message from a genuine one only while H-KEY holds; an attacker with a key is outside this model.

**Implied sub-capabilities (`inferred`).** Passive observation of the BLE traffic within range (a precondition of replay and of the informed level). A relay position on the link: suppression of the genuine messages of a stream, delivery of a genuine message later than it was produced (the time attack of the section Attack classes), and renumbering of later messages. Analisi §4.5 names only injection and replay without keys; the benchmark's replay and informed injection need the relay position (H-SUPP, H-FRESH), so the result of the section Knowledge levels is stated for an attacker in that position. An attacker who can only add messages leaves a repeat or a conflict in the sequence of a device that provides `seq` (TX-1 (b), TX-5 (e)); for a device without `seq` it can override a value without suppression, because the held value is that of the latest device time, ties broken by receive time (T01 section 3.2).

**Hypotheses used by the statements of this document.**

| id | hypothesis |
|---|---|
| H-DEP | Deployment per device: U (no authentication, `crypto_temporal.authenticated` is `None`) or A (authentication). In A a device that authenticates may or may not also provide `seq`. The deployment is per device (TM-OP-8). |
| H-TAG | In A the authentication tag covers the payload, the `seq` and the `t_device_ms` of the message. A tag that covers only the payload leaves the metadata unprotected, and the metadata attacks then behave as in U. |
| H-KEY | Attackers hold no keys. |
| H-GW | The gateway keeps the messages that fail verification in the observed trace and flags them (policy "flag"). Under the policy "drop" a forged message never reaches the monitor, its only effect on the verdicts is the suppression of genuine messages (H-SUPP), and the verification failure is evidence only if it is logged (TM-GAP-AUTH). WS04-T08 does not state the policy; its test 10 (signature exactly `crypto_temporal.authenticated`) reads as "flag" (TM-OP-3). |
| H-SUPP | (relay position, `inferred`) In the benchmark, within the window of a replay or an injection the genuine messages of the targeted stream do not reach the gateway. At the informed level, under H-GW "flag" and with the forged messages substituting for the suppressed ones, this leaves no footprint in the fields derived from the trace; under "drop" the forged messages do not arrive and the delivered sequence has a gap where they were (a loss footprint); at the naive level the footprint of the suppression is not modelled. Whether this is feasible on a real link is not claimed; the case in which both the genuine and the forged messages arrive is handled where it matters (TX-1 (b)). |
| H-SEQSTATE | The monitor retains the accepted `(source, name, seq)` of the previous day (the warm-up prefix of T01 section 5.5 item 4), where `name` is the `signal` of a `Sample` or the kind of an `Event`; without it a replay of the previous day does not repeat an accepted `seq` (TX-1, TM-OP-4). |
| H-FRESH | (relay position, `inferred`) The informed level can keep the `seq` stream and the `t_device_ms` of the targeted device coherent: the delivered `seq` has no gap, duplicate or reorder and the stamps are inside the acceptance bounds. When it inserts an extra message instead of substituting a suppressed one, it must also renumber the later genuine messages of the device (a relay position), otherwise the next genuine message repeats or skips a number (TX-5 (e), TM-OP-7). This is a worst-case definition of the level, not a claim that it is practical. |
| H-CHAN | The available channels are the twenty fields of the `EvidenceVector` contract; no channel gives an independent measurement of the pressure (analisi row 4). "Coherent on every available channel" quantifies over those channels only. |
| H-WIT | The witness channels are produced by the robot, inside the TCB, from its perception. While the robot is not co-located they are `None` and no spoofed value enters them. |
| H-TIME | The monitor has a declared reorder bound `D` (`reorder_delay_ms`) and a declared skew bound `skew_max` (`clock_skew_max_ms`, unset until a human sets it). Statements about a late or a future-stamped input hold relative to them. No value is given here. |
| H-SEQMONO | `seq` is monotone per source and is not reset, wrapped or re-sent by a benign unit. A device restart, a counter wrap, an application-layer re-send, or a `dispenser_bounce` that repeats the real `seq` (FX-6 of `fault-model.md`) each repeat or skip a number without an attacker, and raise the same field (TM-GAP-SEQ). |
| H-TOL | A healthy cuff reports a pulse rate within the coherence tolerance of the ECG rate (the tolerance is a registry name for WS02-T03; no value here). |
| H-AUTHCAUSE | No C2 fault or benign cause makes the verification of a genuine message fail: `fault-model.md` lists no fault that raises `crypto_temporal.authenticated`. A payload corruption, a key mismatch or a firmware error that did make a genuine message fail would raise it without an attacker (TM-OP-15). |
| H-GROUP | A measurement is replayed or forged as the group that the device sends (`bp`, `hr_pulse`, `meas`, `cuff_health`, `inflation_ms`). The informed level writes the whole group as the device does (health 1.0, an inflation value in the device's usual range); at the naive level the SYS instance of a forged measurement reads the held earlier values, or, if the naive attacker writes an arbitrary group, a group that reports an unhealthy cuff or a short inflation and violates φ_SYS (TX-2 (c), G-SYS-2, G-SYS-3). |
| H-EVID | The evidence sources other than the stream the attacker writes are as the devices set them: the `Sample.quality` of an injected message, a DEVICE_ERROR, NODE_STATE or ROBOT_TASK Event, and the system health sources (liveliness, QoS, diagnostics, link metric). In U a keyless injector could in fact write the quality of the Samples it injects, or deliver a device-error message; this evidence steering (excusing a pulse difference, pointing to C2) is not modelled (TM-OP-14). The informed level sets these fields as the device does. |

## Knowledge levels

Two levels of attacker knowledge are defined, as sets of what the attacker knows. Neither level holds keys.

| knowledge item | naive | informed |
|---|---|---|
| message format of the devices (enough to produce messages the gateway parses) | knows | knows |
| care-plan parameters and schedule (times of ticks and dispenses, the limits of the templates) | does not know | knows |
| cross-device invariants (for example the cuff pulse rate against the ECG heart rate at the same instant) | does not know | knows |
| sequence-number and timestamp scheme of the devices | does not know | knows |
| authentication scheme (not the keys) | does not know | knows |
| robot perception model (what makes the robot report a presence) | does not know | knows |
| the patient's own recent readings (by passive capture; `inferred`) | does not know | knows |

The capability flags of the generator follow from the knowledge items. The names are those that WS04-T08 uses (its test 1), so a rename needs a recorded reason.

| flag | naive | informed | knowledge item that grants it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fresh_metadata` | false | true | sequence-number and timestamp scheme (H-FRESH) |
| `coherent_pulse` | false | true | cross-device invariants (and the current ECG heart rate, by passive capture) |
| `plausible_values` | false | true | the patient's own recent readings: false means values drawn from the population physiological bounds that ignore the patient's history, true means values inside the range of the patient's own recent genuine readings |
| `spoofs_witness` | false | true | robot perception model |

The knowledge of the care-plan schedule and of the authentication scheme has no flag: the first decides when the informed level acts (for example at a scheduled dispense, TX-4 (b)), and the second is a property of the deployment (H-DEP), not of the attacker (TM-OP-1). `spoof_intake` is an option of `presence_spoof` at the informed level (WS04-T08 test 9).

**Coherence.** An attacker is coherent on a channel when what it produces yields, on that channel, the value that a genuine event would yield. The informed level is coherent on the channels of the table above that its flags cover. It is not coherent on a channel that needs a key.

**The informed attacker who is coherent on every available channel is not distinguishable by construction** (analisi §4.5, last matrix row, row 10). Mechanism: the fields that these attacks are modelled to raise (`crypto_temporal`, `cross_device`, `witness`) are computed from the observed trace and from the availability of the channels; the other fields are computed from the quality of Samples, from device-error, node-state and robot-task Events, and from system health sources, and H-EVID keeps them as the devices set them. If the observed trace under attack passes every predicate that a genuine trace passes, and those sources are unchanged, no field differs between the two. The statement is for an attacker in the relay position of the section Attacker capabilities. The set of coherent channels depends on the deployment:

- In U, the keyless BLE injection of the informed level is coherent on every available channel under H-CHAN, H-SUPP and H-FRESH. The fields derived from outside the Samples and Events (`system.ble_link_quality`, liveliness) are not determined by the trace, and the informed level is defined as leaving them unchanged (H-SUPP, H-EVID, TM-OP-14). Checks: TX-2, TX-3, TX-9.
- In A, the same attacker is not coherent on `crypto_temporal.authenticated`, because producing a valid tag needs a key (H-KEY, H-TAG). The row-10 statement therefore does not hold for BLE injection in A under H-GW "flag"; the footprint is the single field `crypto_temporal.authenticated` (TX-9).
- Physical access to the dispenser and perception spoofing do not depend on a message the attacker writes, so the authentication of the device does not help against them. The row-10 statement holds for them to the extent stated in their classes (a physical release timed with the schedule, an informed spoofed witness), in both deployments.

This is a declared result, not a defect of the model or of the monitor. It is reported in the diagnosability matrix terms of analisi §4.5 (rows 8, 9 and 10) in the section Attack classes, and the rate at which such events occur is measured by WS08 on the benchmark; no number is written here. Coherence is relative to the twenty fields: an invariant that the monitor does not compute (for example a relation between SpO₂ and heart rate) leaves no evidence whatever the attacker knows.

## Attack classes

The common effect on templates is read from the signal footprint of T02 section 3.1: `replay`, `injection_naive` and `injection_informed` change the `bp` values and the MEASUREMENT events that `range`, `sched`, `hold` and `resp` read, and the group values (`cuff_health`, `inflation_ms`) that `sys` reads; `dispense_forgery` and `dispenser_physical_tamper` add an anchor of `count`, `hold`, `resp` and `int`; `presence_spoof` changes only what `int` reads; `timestamp_manipulation` moves inputs on the grid or out of acceptance. A "can" below holds when the stated condition holds in the trace; it is not a claim about how often. The numbers in the class descriptions are the FIXTURE-ONLY values of the worked checks.

### `replay`

- **Capability and level.** BLE injection and replay without keys. Naive: the message is delivered verbatim with its stale metadata. Informed (`fresh_metadata`): the metadata is rewritten so that `t_device_ms` equals `t_rx_ms` and `seq` continues; with `coherent_pulse` the replayed pulse rate is also patched to the current ECG rate.
- **Injected by WS04-T08.** At the level given by the capability set that the injection receives, recorded in the injection log (both levels).
- **Mechanism.** Earlier genuine messages of a stream (in the benchmark, the previous day's cuff readings, WS04-T08) are delivered again at today's measurement times, while the genuine messages of the window do not reach the gateway (H-SUPP).
- **Preconditions.** A previous passive capture; a previous day (day index above zero); the relay position, or the acceptance that the genuine message also arrives (TX-1 (b)); for the informed level, the knowledge of the section Knowledge levels.
- **Observed-trace change.** `bp` `Sample` values (and `hr_pulse`, `cuff_health`, `inflation_ms`, MEASUREMENT `Event`, H-GROUP) of the previous day at today's times. Naive: `t_device_ms` one day before `t_rx_ms`, `seq` repeating the previous day's. Informed: fresh metadata.
- **Evidence, U, naive: time.** `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` is raised: the replayed input is late, because `t_rx_ms` exceeds the watermark `t_{k_up(t_dev)} + D` of its own step by about a day (T01 section 4.3). Hypotheses: H-TIME with `D` smaller than the age of the message less one step, and the stale stamp kept (naive level). Check: TX-1.
- **Evidence, U, naive: sequence.** A repeat of an accepted `seq` is flagged in `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` only if the device provides `seq`, H-SEQSTATE holds, and the repeat is flagged whether or not the input is also late (TM-OP-4); the gap that the suppressed genuine messages leave in the device's own sequence is not modelled at the naive level (H-SUPP) and would add to it. Check: TX-1, TX-9.
- **Verdict effect, naive.** A late input is not used: the replay does not enter the held `bp` and opens no RANGE or SYS instance. With the genuine reading suppressed the effect is that of a removed measurement (mechanism M-REM of `fault-model.md`: SCHED violated at its decision step, TX-1 (a)); evidence and violation together look like a cuff outage with link evidence. Without suppression the genuine reading is accepted, the replay raises evidence and no violation (TX-1 (b)).
- **Overlap, naive.** The pair of Booleans (timestamp True, sequence True) is also produced by a message loss together with a late input (C2 `message_drop` and `link_delay` at once, TX-1 (c)) and by a benign repeat of a number (H-SEQMONO), because one field mixes a gap and a repeat (TM-GAP-SEQ): the pair does not by itself identify a replay, and a rule that reads it as integrity evidence (step one of ADR-010) would give C3 for a loss with a late input. The replay of the previous day also needs a negative `t_device_ms` in the schema (TM-GAP-HIST, TM-OP-2).
- **Evidence, U, informed.** None from `crypto_temporal`: the input is on time and the `seq` continues (H-FRESH; TX-2, TX-9). `cross_device.pulse_vs_ecg_hr_diff` is raised only if the replayed pulse is not patched and its difference from the ECG rate exceeds the tolerance (TX-2); with `coherent_pulse` nothing is raised. This is the "partial" of analisi row 8 without authentication: the naive replay leaves evidence, the informed replay does not.
- **Evidence, A.** Naive: the same as in U. A verbatim copy of a genuine message carries a valid tag (H-TAG), so authentication does not detect a replay; what can expose it is the sequence numbers and the timestamps, which the tag protects from rewriting. Informed: rewriting `seq` or `t_device_ms` invalidates the tag, so the only adverse field (informed, with the pulse patched) is `crypto_temporal.authenticated` (False; H-KEY, H-TAG, H-GW "flag"; the tag state is an input of TX-9, so the check restates the hypotheses). This is the "yes with authentication" of analisi row 8, under those hypotheses and subject to TM-GAP-SEQ.
- **Violations.** The naive replay can make `sched`, `hold`, `resp` violated by removing the genuine measurement (M-REM; TX-1 (a) for `sched`). The informed replay can change `range`, `hold`, `resp` through the replayed value in either direction: a replayed in-band value masks a latent violation (TX-2: latent 152, replayed 128) and a replayed out-of-band value creates one where the latent value is in band (TX-2, reverse); the instance of `sys` reads the replayed group and is satisfied when the group is healthy (TX-2 (c)). It can mask a true C1 event, and an informed replay of a healthy group can mask a C2 cuff-health event (`sys`).
- **Diagnosability reading (row 8).** Under authentication (H-KEY, H-TAG, H-GW "flag", H-AUTHCAUSE) the informed replay leaves evidence, a failed verification; the verbatim naive replay is as in U, partial, because a verbatim copy has a valid tag. Without authentication the naive replay leaves evidence (with the false-C3 hazard above) and the informed replay does not.

### `injection_naive`

- **Capability and level.** BLE injection and replay without keys, naive level: `plausible_values`, `coherent_pulse` and `fresh_metadata` all false.
- **Injected by WS04-T08.** At the naive level.
- **Mechanism.** Forged messages whose values lie inside the physiological bounds (T05 domain of `bp`), chosen without the patient's history, delivered in place of the genuine messages of the window (H-SUPP).
- **Preconditions.** Radio range; the message format; the relay position, or the acceptance that the genuine message also arrives.
- **Observed-trace change.** `bp` `Sample` and MEASUREMENT `Event` with forged values; `hr_pulse` independent of the ECG; `seq` not following the device's scheme; `t_device_ms` as the attacker chose it; the rest of the group as H-GROUP says.
- **Evidence, U.** Three fields can be raised, each under a condition. `cross_device.pulse_vs_ecg_hr_diff`, if the ECG heart rate is available at the instant (otherwise it is `None`) and the difference exceeds the tolerance. `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay`, if the device provides `seq` and the forged number leaves a gap or repeats one (TX-9: a number that jumps ahead leaves a gap). `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`, by the injected messages only if the stamp the attacker wrote lies outside the acceptance bounds (H-TIME): a stamp inside them is accepted and unflagged. A naive injection whose pulse is within the tolerance by chance, whose device has no `seq` and whose stamp is inside the bounds leaves no evidence. The ECG noise fault of C2 (`ecg_noise`) can also raise `cross_device.pulse_vs_ecg_hr_diff`, so that field alone does not say attack (section The C1, C2 and C3 boundary, TM-OP-12).
- **Evidence, A.** As in U, and `crypto_temporal.authenticated` is False for every forged message (H-KEY, H-TAG, H-GW "flag"). Under H-GW "drop" the forged messages never reach the monitor and the effect is that of a suppressed stream.
- **Violations.** Plausible values can leave `range`, `hold`, `resp` satisfied or violated; the physiological bounds are wider than the care-plan band (analisi §4.1 and §4.7, illustrative, unverified), so a violation is possible. The SYS instance of a forged measurement reads the group it carries or, at the naive level, the held earlier values (TX-2 (c)); a forged group that reports an unhealthy cuff violates φ_SYS (G-SYS-3). When a violation is raised and a field above is raised, the integrity-first hierarchy of ADR-010 can read it; when none is raised the violation reaches attribution with coherent channels, where the third step of the default rule of ADR-010 reads it as C1 (WS02-T04 fixes the conditions).
- **Diagnosability reading.** No row of the analisi matrix. Conditional on the cross-device evidence with the signal quality available, and on the `seq` and the stamp of the injected messages.

### `injection_informed`

- **Capability and level.** BLE injection and replay without keys, informed level: all four flags true.
- **Injected by WS04-T08.** At the informed level, tagged `expected_undetectable` when its evidence signature is empty (TM-OP-8).
- **Mechanism.** Forged messages with values inside the range of the patient's own recent readings, fresh metadata, the cuff pulse rate set equal to the current ECG rate, the whole group written as the device does (H-GROUP), timed with the care-plan schedule, substituting the genuine messages of the window (H-SUPP, H-FRESH).
- **Preconditions.** Passive capture of the patient's traffic (readings and the ECG rate); the relay position; the knowledge of the informed level.
- **Observed-trace change.** `bp` and MEASUREMENT `Event` of the window replaced, with fresh `t_device_ms` and continuing `seq`, `hr_pulse` equal to `hr_ecg` at each injected instant, `cuff_health` and `inflation_ms` as the device reports them.
- **Evidence, U.** None. Mechanism: the metadata passes the acceptance rules (T01 sections 4.2 to 4.4), the `seq` has no gap or repeat, the pulse difference is zero, the quality and group fields are as the device sets them (H-EVID, H-GROUP), and no other field reads the pressure (H-CHAN). A flat or repeated series would be a further cue, but no field carries it (FM-GAP-FLAT of `fault-model.md`). This is row 10 of the analisi matrix: not distinguishable by construction, a declared result, for an attacker in the relay position. Checks: TX-9 (no field raised); TX-3 (an informed series equal to the frozen value of a `stuck_at`, or to the latent value plus the offset of a `cuff_silent_bias` and inside the patient's own range, has the same `bp` Samples, MEASUREMENT Events, timing, `seq` and `quality` as the fault, by the two signatures; the `hr_pulse` Sample of the injection equals the ECG rate while the fault leaves the genuine pulse, which differs by the cuff's own pulse error, so the two have the same evidence readings while a healthy device stays inside the coherence tolerance, H-TOL).
- **Evidence, A.** `crypto_temporal.authenticated` only (TX-9, an input-driven check), under H-KEY, H-TAG and H-GW "flag"; with a key it would be the compromised-device case, which is excluded.
- **Violations.** Values inside the patient's own range are not necessarily inside the band: a forged 150, inside the range of a patient whose recent readings span 135 to 152 (FIXTURE-ONLY), violates `range` (TX-3). On `resp` it can hide a non-response (forged 138 where the latent 145 violates, `resp` satisfied) and it can produce an apparent non-response (forged 150 where the latent 132 satisfies, `resp` violated); both look like a clinical deviation or its absence (TX-3). It can leave every template satisfied (section Attacks that raise no violation).
- **Diagnosability reading (row 10).** Not distinguishable, in U, for an attacker in the relay position; in A, under H-KEY, H-TAG, H-GW "flag" and H-AUTHCAUSE, the field `crypto_temporal.authenticated` makes the BLE form distinguishable (TM-OP-11).

### `dispense_forgery`

- **Capability and level.** BLE injection and replay without keys. Naive: the message has no valid tag and non-coherent metadata. Informed: fresh metadata (H-FRESH) and the schedule knowledge; with `spoofs_witness` it is combined with `presence_spoof` (WS04-T09 test 14).
- **Injected by WS04-T08.** At the level given by the capability set, recorded in the injection log; synthesised only.
- **Mechanism.** A DISPENSE `Event` that the dispenser did not produce is delivered: synthesised, or a copy of a captured genuine one. In the benchmark the event is synthesised (WS04-T08 test 8).
- **Preconditions.** Radio range; the message format; for a copy, a previous capture.
- **Observed-trace change.** One extra DISPENSE `Event` with the dispenser's source and an attacker-chosen `t_device_ms`; no `auth` `Sample`; no INTAKE; the latent trace unchanged (no dose was released).
- **Templates anchored on it.** `count`, `hold`, `resp`, `int` (T02 section 3.1). Effects (TX-4): `count` violated for an unscheduled extra dose (G-COUNT-1 arithmetic) and a missed day-end dose masked by a forged dispense at the scheduled time (G-COUNT-3 arithmetic); `hold` violated when the last reading is below the threshold, by a dose that was never released (G-HOLD-2 arithmetic); `resp` violated or satisfied at the end of its response window depending on the pressure that the cuff reports, which no dose has influenced (G-RESP-2 and G-RESP-3 arithmetic); `int` as below.
- **`int`, robot co-located, no authentication (U, or A: the forgery has no valid tag).** If the `presence` level is held as 0 and no `auth` exists, the instance is VIOLATED with the guard `no_witness` (V-INT-3). A violation of φ_INT is context, not an attribution: a genuine dispense that the robot did not witness gives the same instance (a patient who left, or a perception false negative, OP-1 of `fault-model.md`). If the real patient is in the room and the robot sees them, `presence` is 1 and the instance is SATISFIED although the dispense is forged: INT tests that someone was seen, not that the person caused the dispense.
- **`int`, robot not co-located.** Both witness channels are `None`, and with no `auth` the instance stays PENDING with a marker (V-INT-1); it is not violated. The forgery then raises no violation of φ_INT.
- **`int`, informed with a spoofed witness.** A `presence` sample (or an INTAKE event) inside the window makes the instance SATISFIED (G-INT-1 arithmetic, TX-4 (c), TX-6).
- **Evidence, U.** `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` can be raised at the naive level, if the device provides `seq` and the forged number leaves a gap or repeats one (H-SEQMONO). The informed level keeps the `seq` coherent (H-FRESH), so nothing is raised (TX-9). `witness.robot_present` and `witness.intake_observed` read False while the robot is co-located and saw nothing (TX-4 (c)), True if it saw the patient, and are `None` otherwise. No field says that the dispense was forged: an informed forgery with a spoofed witness that includes `spoof_intake`, or with the robot absent, gives the same observed stream as a real dispense after which the patient does not respond (WS04-T09 test 14, confounder `nonresponse_vs_falsified_dispense`).
- **Evidence, A, synthesised.** A synthesised forgery has no valid tag: `crypto_temporal.authenticated` is False on the violated instances that the forged event anchors (`count`, `hold`, `resp`, `int`), at both levels (H-KEY, H-TAG, H-GW "flag"; TX-9 restates the hypotheses), where the anchoring message belongs to the evidence window of the instance (WS02-T03 D-7, the evidence cut-off).
- **Evidence, A, copied.** A copied authentic message carries a valid tag: `crypto_temporal.authenticated` is clean. Among the `crypto_temporal` fields only a repeated `seq`, if the device provides it (TX-5 (a)), or lateness beyond `D` can expose the copy; from a device without `seq`, within `D`, it is an extra DISPENSE that is counted (TX-5 (b)).
- **Violations and the absence of one.** Whether the forgery raises any violation depends on the limits and on the pressure; it can raise none (section Attacks that raise no violation).
- **Diagnosability reading (row 9).** Partial (analisi: no embodied witness; the response violated after Δ2). Refinement `inferred`: in A, under H-KEY, H-TAG, H-GW "flag" and H-AUTHCAUSE, the synthesised forgery is distinguished by `crypto_temporal.authenticated` wherever a violation exists to attribute.

### `presence_spoof`

- **Capability and level.** Perception spoofing. Naive: a stimulus that yields `presence` but not an identity. Informed (`spoofs_witness`): the stimulus matches the perception model, so identity (where the robot checks it) and, with `spoof_intake`, an intake are also reported.
- **Injected by WS04-T08.** At the level given by the capability set, with the option `spoof_intake` at the informed level.
- **Mechanism.** The robot, unmodified, reports what its sensors were made to perceive. A bystander, who is no attacker, gives the same output (presence is not identity, R3 weakness 5).
- **Preconditions.** The robot is co-located with its witness channels available (H-WIT); the attacker is in its sensing range.
- **Observed-trace change.** A `presence` `Sample` of value 1.0 and optionally an INTAKE `Event` (source robot) that the latent trace does not contain. The messages are genuine robot outputs: sequence numbers and timestamps are the robot's, and no device authentication is involved.
- **Evidence.** In U and in A the same: no `crypto_temporal` field. `witness.robot_present` reads True and `witness.intake_observed` True (informed with `spoof_intake`); `witness.identity_confirmed` can be False at the naive level, only if the robot performs an identity check (WS02-T03 decides whether and how); at the informed level nothing contradicts the spoof. No field says how the observation was obtained, so nothing can contradict an informed spoof by a field of the contract (declared limit; section Non-claims, TM-OP-13).
- **Violations.** None, alone: the only template that reads `presence` or `intake` is `int`, and adding a witness observation can only move an instance of `int` upward (a violation or a pending instance can become satisfied, never the reverse). Lemma: the body of `int` is a maximum over the steps of the minimum of a witness term and a margin; the witness term is a disjunction, so adding a true value raises a term and no term falls. Hypotheses: H-WIT, spoofed values of 1 only, the reference form of INT (TM-OP-9). Check: TX-6 (a) and the seeded property check of TX-6 (b), which confirms the lemma on the evaluator. What the spoof can do is mask: it turns the violation of φ_INT of a forged or unwitnessed dispense into a satisfied instance (TX-6 (c), (d)), and it changes the evidence of the violations that others raise (a spoofed INTAKE makes a forged dispense followed by a non-response look like a genuine non-response, C1, WS04-T09 test 14).
- **Diagnosability reading.** No row of the analisi matrix; derived: not distinguishable at the informed level in either deployment, partial at the naive level only through `witness.identity_confirmed`, and in no case through attribution when it raises no violation.

### `dispenser_physical_tamper`

- **Capability and level.** Physical access to the dispenser; levels do not apply (the event is a genuine device output; the schedule knowledge decides when). Basis `inferred`; modelled, not injected in benchmark v1 unless WS04-T08 decides otherwise.
- **Injected by WS04-T08.** Not injected in benchmark v1 (modelled only).
- **Mechanism.** An actor other than the patient makes the dispenser release a dose at a time the care plan and the patient did not cause. The device reports it faithfully.
- **Observed-trace change.** One extra DISPENSE `Event` with the device's own `seq`, fresh timestamps and, where the device authenticates, a valid `auth` `Sample`; no INTAKE.
- **Evidence.** None in `crypto_temporal`, in U or in A: the event is genuine (H-SEQMONO, H-TAG). Where the robot is co-located and nobody is present, `witness.robot_present` and `witness.intake_observed` read False on the violated instances that the release anchors (`count`, `hold`, `resp`; TX-7); that combination is also produced by a patient who opened the dispenser and was not seen (C1, or a perception false negative). An authenticated event proves the origin device, not the intake and not the actor (ADR-004 keeps DISPENSE and INTAKE apart). A co-located robot that checks identity can contradict the release through `witness.identity_confirmed` if it sees someone else (WS02-T03 D-6); an informed spoofed witness removes even that.
- **`int`.** In A, the authenticated `auth` sample satisfies `int` whatever the witness reads: INT is SATISFIED with the robot co-located and `presence` held as 0 (V-INT-2 arithmetic, TX-7 (a)). In U, with no `auth`, no witness and the robot co-located, `int` is VIOLATED as for a forgery (TX-7 (b)); with the robot away it is PENDING. Whether `auth` should mask the witness is a template question (TM-OP-10).
- **Violations.** `count` can be violated at the release or at a later genuine dispense inside the trailing day (TX-7 (c)); `hold`, `resp` as for a forgery.
- **Diagnosability reading.** No row of the analisi matrix. Not distinguishable from the patient's own behaviour by the `crypto_temporal` fields or by `provenance` (C1 against C3 by cause, section The C1, C2 and C3 boundary); physical access defeats authentication; among the twenty fields only a co-located robot that checks identity can contradict it.

### `timestamp_manipulation`

- **Capability and level.** BLE injection and replay without keys, applied to the time metadata; basis `inferred` (analisi §4.9 item 2: time is also an attack vector); modelled, not injected in benchmark v1 unless WS04-T08 decides otherwise. Informed: the stamp is placed inside the acceptance bounds; naive: outside.
- **Injected by WS04-T08.** Not injected in benchmark v1 (modelled only).
- **Mechanism.** A genuine message is delivered with a rewritten `t_device_ms`, or delayed, so that its position on the grid or its acceptance changes (M-SKEW and M-LATE of `fault-model.md`). It needs the relay position.
- **Observed-trace change.** `t_device_ms` displaced, or `t_rx_ms` increased, for one device's messages; values and `seq` unchanged.
- **Evidence, U.** Under the reading above, `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` is raised only when the input is late (`t_rx_ms` after `t_{k_up(t_dev)} + D`) or future-stamped (`t_dev` above `t_rx` plus `skew_max`); a displacement or a delay inside the bounds is accepted, unflagged, and can change a verdict (TX-8 (a): a measurement taken inside the SCHED window and stamped two steps later is read after the deadline; SCHED violated, nothing counted). A delay inside the reorder bound changes no verdict and leaves no evidence (TX-8 (d)).
- **Evidence, A.** Rewriting `t_device_ms` invalidates the tag (H-TAG): `crypto_temporal.authenticated` is False. A delay of a verbatim message keeps the tag valid: only lateness beyond `D` shows, through `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`.
- **Violations.** `sched` (and `hold`, through the same displacement) violated; `range` and `resp` masked for a message that is late.
- **Diagnosability reading.** No row of the analisi matrix. Within the bounds not distinguishable from a clock skew or a delay (C2); beyond them the evidence exists and the cause does not follow from it (next subsection).

### Time as an attack vector and the C2 time faults

The C2 time faults are `link_delay`, `out_of_order` and `clock_skew` of `fault-model.md`; the attack side is `replay` and `timestamp_manipulation`. The boundary is by cause. The table gives the observable overlap, and what in the trace could separate them. "Separable" means a field computed from the trace can differ between the two causes under the stated hypotheses (H-SEQMONO among them).

| symptom in the trace | C2 or benign cause | attack cause | what the monitor sees | separable by the trace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a late input (beyond `D`) | `link_delay` beyond the bound; `clock_skew` behind; a device that buffers messages and forwards them later | `replay` with stale metadata; `timestamp_manipulation` by delay | counted, not used, `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` | a stale-stamped replay is separable from a delay only through `seq`: a replay repeats an accepted `seq`, a delayed genuine message carries a new one; this needs `seq`, H-SEQSTATE, H-SEQMONO and a field that separates a repeat from a gap (TM-GAP-SEQ); without `seq`, identical (TX-1). A delay by an attacker (`timestamp_manipulation`) and a link delay are the same Event, with the same new `seq`: no field separates them (TX-8 (c)) |
| a future-stamped input | `clock_skew` ahead beyond `skew_max` | `timestamp_manipulation`, stamp forward | not used, counted | no: one Boolean covers late, future-stamped and replay-related inputs (TM-GAP-TIME); TX-8 (b) |
| a stamp displaced inside the bounds | `clock_skew` within `skew_max` | `timestamp_manipulation`, informed | accepted, unflagged, verdict shifted | no evidence exists (TX-8 (a), FX-4) |
| a delay inside the reorder bound | `link_delay` or `out_of_order` within `D` | a relay that delays within `D` | absorbed; no evidence; no verdict change | identical (TX-8 (d), V-LATE-2) |
| a repeated `seq` | a device restart or counter reset; an application-layer re-send; a `dispenser_bounce` that repeats the real `seq` (FX-6 of `fault-model.md`) | a replayed copy; an inserted message that takes a number the device later uses (TX-5 (e)) | a repeated `(source, seq)` is a duplicate, not counted and flagged | no: the same field and the same Events (TM-GAP-SEQ, H-SEQMONO) |
| a duplicate or an extra DISPENSE | `dispenser_bounce` (own `seq`) | a verbatim or re-stamped copy within `D`; a synthesised `dispense_forgery` | with `seq`: a repeated `(source, seq)` is a duplicate, not counted and flagged (a copy); a forged event whose `seq` continues the device's is counted and unflagged at that moment, as a bounce with its own `seq` is; without `seq` an extra DISPENSE is counted | a verbatim copy is separable from a bounce only with `seq`, or by its repeated `t_device_ms`, which the duplicate policy does not use; a re-stamped copy or a forgery with a continuing `seq`, or from a device without `seq`, gives the same Events as a bounce (for a dispenser without content sensing, FM-GAP-DISP), and with a continuing `seq` the device's next genuine message then repeats the number unless it is renumbered (TX-5 (a) to (e)) |
| arrival after newer messages | `out_of_order` | a replayed copy that arrives later | absorbed within the bound; beyond it, late | see rows 1 and 4 |
| absent messages | `message_drop`, `ble_dropout` | suppression, a component of replay and injection in the relay position (H-SUPP); not an attack class (jamming as an availability attack is excluded) | Samples and Events absent; a `seq` gap where `seq` is present | the Samples and Events of the stream are those of `message_drop`; `ble_dropout` also changes `system.node_lifecycle`, which suppression does not (H-EVID); fields derived from outside the trace (`system.ble_link_quality`, liveliness) are not determined by it |

In the cases marked identical, or with no evidence, in the table the monitor cannot separate the two causes by symptom alone: a replay within the reorder bound can look like a duplicate (from a device without `seq` it is an extra DISPENSE, like a bounce), and a delayed message can be a link fault or an attack. The benchmark separates them by cause (the ground truth). One C2 fault and one attack that coincide in time are not modelled (`fault-model.md`: a fault and an attack at once).

### The C1, C2 and C3 boundary

- **C1 against C3.** A patient who takes an extra dose, refuses, or diverts a dose is adherence deviation, C1 (analisi §4.5: the double take of the patient is a deviation of adherence, not a fault and not an attack). A release by another actor (`dispenser_physical_tamper`) and a patient's own release leave the same genuine, authenticated DISPENSE; the trace does not identify the actor. A forged dispense followed by no response and a real non-response (WS04 confounder `nonresponse_vs_falsified_dispense`) have observed streams that differ in these cases: the robot is co-located and the attacker is naive (the forged variant has no INTAKE, WS04-T09 test 15); a synthesised forgery in A (no valid tag); a naive forgery from a device that provides `seq` (the number leaves a gap). They are equal with a spoofed witness that includes `spoof_intake` and coherent metadata in U, and with the robot absent and coherent metadata in U (WS04-T09 test 14, TM-OP-5). An informed injection that looks like a hypertensive reading or a non-response is C1 by symptom and C3 by cause (TX-3).
- **C2 against C3.** Faults that look like attacks: `stuck_at` and `cuff_silent_bias` against `injection_informed` (the `bp` Samples and the MEASUREMENT Events are equal for a series chosen at the frozen value or at the latent value plus the offset, and the evidence readings are equal under H-TOL, TX-3; analisi rows 4 and 10 are both declared limits); `dispenser_bounce` against a re-stamped copy or a forged DISPENSE from a device without `seq` (TX-5 (c)); a perception false negative or positive against `presence_spoof` and against a bystander (OP-1 of `fault-model.md`); `ecg_noise` against a naive injection through `cross_device.pulse_vs_ecg_hr_diff` (the evidence confound of `fault-model.md`; `signal_quality.ecg_sqi` marks the noise); a benign repeat of a `seq` against a replay (H-SEQMONO); a verification failure of a genuine message (payload corruption, a key mismatch, a firmware error) against a forged message (H-AUTHCAUSE, TM-OP-15). Attacks that look like faults: a stale-stamped replay with the genuine reading suppressed against a cuff outage (TX-1 (a)); suppression, which as a component of replay or injection leaves the footprint of a loss and is not an attack class of its own (it matches `message_drop`; `ble_dropout` also changes the node state and liveliness, which a relay position does not, unless evidence steering is allowed, H-EVID).
- **Provenance is not an attack indicator.** A violated φ_INT instance can come from an attack (`dispense_forgery`, unauthenticated release), from a C1 cause (a patient who left) and from C2 causes (a lost or late `presence` sample, CAS-5; a duplicate instance of `dispenser_bounce`, OP-12 of `fault-model.md`). The attribution rule of WS02-T04 requires at least one asserted field outside `provenance`.

### Worked checks (fixture-only)

All values are FIXTURE-ONLY (registry F0, `T_s` one minute): `D` is 30000 and `skew_max` is 180000 where used, pressures in the examples are plain numbers, and times are device times. Each check was computed by hand and reproduced by an integer-millisecond evaluator written for this task, first validated on 46 golden and worked cases (all golden cases of `templates.yaml`, the `V-` cases it covers, FX-3 and FX-4 of `fault-model.md`, the duplicate and multiplicity rules of T01). The evaluator is not committed and its outputs are in the notes of the task file. Some checks derive a statement from the definition of two signatures or of the schema (TX-3, TX-5 (c), TX-8 (c)) and some set an input from a hypothesis (the tag state in TX-9); they are marked, and none of them is an independent test of the monitor. Previous-day readings are 128 at 08:05 and 152 at 12:05; the tick is 08:00 (`k_a` 480, `k_d` 510).

### TX-1 Naive replay on SCHED

The previous day's 08:05 message (`bp` 128, MEASUREMENT, `seq` 21) is delivered at 08:05 today with its stale stamp (one day earlier). (a) Genuine reading suppressed: both inputs are late (the watermark of their own step passed a day earlier) and repeat an accepted `(source, name, seq)` of the previous day (H-SEQSTATE); neither enters the accepted trace, so no `meas` lies in `[480, 510]` and SCHED is VIOLATED at 08:30 with `no_measurement_after_tick` (V-SCHED-3); no RANGE instance exists. `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` is True and `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` True. (b) The genuine reading (08:05, `seq` 24) is also delivered: SCHED is SATISFIED with slack 1500000 (G-SCHED-1 arithmetic); the two Booleans are still raised and no violation exists. (c) A message loss (`seq` 22 missing between 21 and 23) together with one late input (a link delay beyond `D`) gives the same two Booleans, True and True. Without the previous day's `seq` state only the timestamp Boolean remains (the gap left by the suppressed genuine messages is not modelled, H-SUPP); from a device without `seq` the sequence field is `None`, not False.

### TX-2 Informed replay on RANGE, SCHED and SYS

The replayed 08:05 value 128 is delivered at 08:05 with `t_device_ms` equal to `t_rx_ms` and `seq` 24. (a) Latent reading 152: RANGE is VIOLATED at -12 (G-RANGE-2); with the replayed 128 it is SATISFIED at +12, a masked violation. SCHED has slack 1500000 either way. Reverse at 12:05: the replayed 152 where the latent reading is 125 gives -12 where the latent value gives +15 (G-RANGE-1). (b) Evidence: `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` False, `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` False; the replayed pulse 64 against an ECG rate 80 exceeds a fixture tolerance of 5, so `cross_device.pulse_vs_ecg_hr_diff` is raised; with the pulse patched to 80 it is not, and no reading is raised. (c) A forged or replayed MEASUREMENT also opens a SYS instance. With the whole group replayed (`cuff_health` 1.0, `inflation_ms` 45000) it is SATISFIED at 15000 (G-SYS-1 arithmetic); a forged measurement without its group reads an earlier genuine group if one is held, SATISFIED at 15000 if that group is healthy with `inflation_ms` 45000, and with none it is PENDING with `value_undefined:cuff_health` and `value_undefined:inflation_ms` (T05 section 3.5); a forged group that reports an unhealthy cuff is VIOLATED with `cuff_unhealthy` (G-SYS-3).

### TX-3 Informed injection on RESP and RANGE

The patient's recent genuine readings span 135 to 152 (FIXTURE-ONLY history for `plausible_values`). Pre-dose 150 at 06:55, dispense 07:00, response window `[08:00, 10:00]`. Latent non-response 145 at 08:30: RESP VIOLATED at -5 (G-RESP-2). Forged 138: SATISFIED at +2 (G-RESP-3), a non-response hidden. Latent response 132: +8 (G-RESP-1). Forged 150: VIOLATED at -10, an apparent non-response; the same forged 150 gives RANGE -10 (the patient's range is not the band). ### TX-5 A replayed or synthesised DISPENSE against a bounce

Real dispenses 07:00 (`seq` 5) and 21:00 (`seq` 6), `D` 30000. (a) A verbatim copy of the 21:00 message (same `t_device_ms`, same `seq`) received ten seconds later: on time, a duplicate, not counted (count 2, margin 0, SATISFIED), one repeat flagged. (b) The same verbatim copy from a device without `seq`: on time, counted, count 3 at that step, so the COUNT instance of the real 21:00 dispense is VIOLATED at -1 as well; nothing flagged, nothing late. (c) By the definition of the schema (derivation): a re-stamped copy (`t_device_ms` equal to `t_rx_ms`, the informed level) from a device without `seq` and a `dispenser_bounce` event (an extra DISPENSE shortly after, no `seq`) are the same Event field for every field of the trace schema, for a dispenser without content sensing (a content-change key in the payload would differ, FM-GAP-DISP); the verbatim copy of (b) differs in `t_device_ms`, which equals the original's, a cue that the duplicate policy of T01 section 3.4 does not use. (d) The verbatim copy arrives within `D` of its own step, so it is on time. (e) With the real 07:00 (`seq` 5) and 21:00 (`seq` 6), an inserted DISPENSE at 21:02 that takes `seq` 7 makes the device's own next DISPENSE (23:00, `seq` 7) a duplicate: flagged and not counted (T01 section 3.4); the count at 23:01 is 3, where it would be 4 if the genuine dose were counted.

### TX-6 Presence spoof

(a) Without a DISPENSE anchor no INT instance exists, so a spoof alone changes nothing. (b) Property check of the lemma of the class description: over 4000 seeded random traces (dispense time, presence samples, an optional INTAKE or `auth`, an optional unavailable interval) adding one or two `presence` samples of 1 never lowered either end of the INT interval and never turned a non-VIOLATED instance into VIOLATED; 972 verdicts changed, all upward; spoofed samples were not placed in unavailable steps (H-WIT). ## Non-claims

1. C3 is not inferred from low ρ, noise, distribution shift or faults, which also produce low ρ (R2 weakness 7). ρ answers how confidently the violation is claimed, and κ answers why; κ is computed from evidence fields (ADR-010), never from ρ.
2. An authenticated dispense event proves the origin device, not INTAKE and not the actor (ADR-004 keeps DISPENSE and INTAKE apart): physical access defeats authentication, in the sense of the assumption on keys, and a verbatim copy of an authentic message carries a valid tag.
3. Robot presence is not identity (R3 weakness 5): a bystander gives the same `presence` as the patient, and the witness fields carry no record of how the observation was obtained.
4. No claim is made about detecting the informed attacker who is coherent on every available channel: by construction no field separates it (analisi row 10), and where this document says that a field is raised it is a different attacker (the naive level, which lacks the knowledge, or one that cannot be coherent on a channel that needs a key) or a different deployment. The result is stated for an attacker in the relay position; an attacker who can only add messages is not covered by it.
5. No general adversarial detection is claimed: the claim is limited to integrity or plausibility violations against the attack classes of this document, under the hypotheses H-* stated, and says nothing about an attack class that is not listed.
6. Provenance alone is context and never sufficient to attribute C3 (WS02-T04): a violated φ_INT instance is not an attribution, and C1 and C2 causes can produce it (section The C1, C2 and C3 boundary, OP-12 and CAS-5 of `fault-model.md`).
7. No claim about rates: how often an attack class leaves evidence, or none, is measured by WS08 on the benchmark.
8. No claim that the declared attack classes are the realistic ones for a ward, no claim of security assurance for a deployment, and no claim implying clinical validation: the validation is technical and pre-clinical.
9. No claim that the evidence cannot be steered: in U a keyless injector could write the quality of the Samples it injects or deliver a device-error message (H-EVID, TM-OP-14); the document models the evidence as the devices set it.
10. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence: a field that is `None` (channel unavailable) is never converted to a negative observation, so the lack of `witness` evidence with the robot away says nothing about an attack.

## Attacks that raise no violation

Assumption: attribution runs only on violated verdicts. The `Attributor` of the Attribution contract takes the records that the monitor reports as violated (and, when the predictive channel is on, the predicted violations), and no alert exists for a record that is SATISFIED or PENDING (TM-OP-15 records the tension with T05 section 3.5 on a PENDING instance with a marker). An attack whose injected data satisfies every template, or leaves an instance PENDING, therefore raises no alert, and WS08 counts it as an undetected C3 event in the detection metric, not as an attribution error: nothing was attributed. A violation that the attack does raise and that is attributed to another class is an attribution error (C3 read as C1), counted in the attribution confusion. The ground truth contains the C3 event in both cases (analisi §5.1). The events that WS02-T05 expects as C1 or NO_ALERT (the informed injection, row 10) and that WS04-T08 tags `expected_undetectable` are the declared limit and are reported separately from the attribution errors; the tag belongs to the events whose evidence signature is empty (TM-OP-8).

An attack can also mask a violation that exists on the latent trace (a missed C1 event); both events are in the ground truth and the accounting is WS08's.

| subtype | can raise no violation when | check |
|---|---|---|
| `replay` | the informed replay carries in-band values and a healthy group (TX-2), or the naive replay is delivered without suppression, so the genuine reading is accepted (TX-1 (b)) | TX-1, TX-2 |
| `injection_naive` | the forged values are inside the care-plan band and above the hold threshold, so that `range` and `hold` are satisfied (G-RANGE-1, G-HOLD-1), and the group read by `sys` is healthy or held (TX-2 (c), G-SYS-1) | G-RANGE-1, TX-2 |
| `injection_informed` | by definition the values are inside the patient's own range, and a value inside the band satisfies `range`; an in-range value can also hide a non-response (TX-3) | TX-3 |
| `dispense_forgery` | the robot is not co-located and there is no `auth` (INT is PENDING, not violated, TX-4 (c)), the count is inside the limit, the hold reading is above the threshold and the pressure falls anyway (TX-4 (d)); or a spoofed witness satisfies INT | TX-4 |
| `presence_spoof` | alone, in every trace under H-WIT and the reference form of INT: it cannot make any template violated, it can only mask (TX-6 (a), (b)) | TX-6 |
| `dispenser_physical_tamper` | the authenticated release satisfies INT (TX-7 (a)) and the count, hold and response instances are satisfied | TX-7 |
| `timestamp_manipulation` | the displacement is inside the bounds and the verdict does not change, or the delay is inside `D` (TX-8 (d)) | TX-8 |

All seven subtypes can behave this way; for none of them does the document claim that a violation is raised.

### Contract gaps (not changed here)

The contract of `docs/plan/02-interfaces.md` is not edited. The model needs the following, recorded for the owning tasks.

- **TM-GAP-AUTH.** The trace schema has no representation of a per-message verification outcome (`Sample` and `Event` carry no flag, `EventKind` has no member for a gateway outcome, and `SignalId.AUTH` is the authenticated dispense event only), and the contract does not say what the adverse value of `crypto_temporal.authenticated` is. The footprint in the column A of every attack that forges or rewrites a message depends on it; WS04-T08 uses a provisional gateway Event key; WS02-T03 gap G-4.
- **TM-GAP-SEQ.** `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` mixes a repeat (replay, a C3 symptom) and a gap (loss, a C2 symptom), so the pair of Booleans of a naive replay is also produced by a loss with a late input. The same gap as FM-GAP-SEQ of `fault-model.md`; WS02-T03 decision D-3.
- **TM-GAP-SEQKEY.** The duplicate rule of T01 section 3.4 is stated for events by `(source, seq)`; a `Sample` carries `seq` but no `source` field, so a duplicate rule for samples needs a key (this document uses the `signal`).
- **TM-GAP-TIME.** `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` is one Boolean for a late input, a future-stamped input and a replay-related one, and there is no clock-health field: the same gap as CG-10, which `fault-model.md` cites from T01.
- **TM-GAP-HIST.** The contract has no previous-day history for a day-by-day run (`run_offline(day, ...)`, `ObservedDay`) and does not define a negative `TimeMs`; the replay of the previous day needs both (CG-11 of T01; H-SEQSTATE).

### Open points

- **TM-OP-1 Knowledge without a flag.** The knowledge of the care-plan schedule and of the authentication scheme has no flag in WS04-T08 (its four flags are `fresh_metadata`, `coherent_pulse`, `plausible_values`, `spoofs_witness`); here the first decides the time of an informed attack and the second is a deployment property. The mapping of the six knowledge items to the four flags, and the meaning of `plausible_values` (the patient's own range), are proposals for WS04-T08 to confirm.
- **TM-OP-2 Replay values and stale metadata.** WS04-T08 test 2 expects today's `bp` at 08:00, 12:00, 16:00 to equal the replayed values and its test 4 gives the naive replay a `t_device_ms` one day earlier. Under the merged semantics a stale-stamped replay is late and not accepted (TX-1), so the equality holds on `t_rx_ms` and in the observed stream, not in the accepted trace; the test should state which.
- **TM-OP-3 Gateway policy.** The policy "flag" (H-GW) is assumed; under "drop" an injection changes the verdicts only by suppression and the verification failure is evidence only if logged. The task WS02-T03 and WS04-T08 should record the policy.
- **TM-OP-4 Sequence state across days and the order of the duplicate and late checks.** H-SEQSTATE assumes that the accepted `(source, name, seq)` of the previous day is retained, and the evaluator flags a repeat whether or not the input is also late. T01 section 3.4 fixes neither (T01 section 5.5 item 4 gives the warm-up prefix, and CG-11 the negative time).
- **TM-OP-5 Authentication in the confounder pairs.** WS04-T09 test 14 (equal observed streams for a spoofed forgery and a real non-response) holds in U; in A the synthesised forgery has no valid tag and the streams differ in the verification outcome.
- **TM-OP-6 The simulation.** WS07-T03 maps the trace items with `seq`, `t_device_ms` and `quality` and carries no verification outcome, and WS07-T05 injects C2 only: the C3 rows and the authenticated deployment are benchmark-only in v1.
- **TM-OP-7 Informed forgery of an extra event.** An informed forged DISPENSE that keeps `seq` coherent needs the renumbering of the device's later messages (H-FRESH, TX-5 (e)); WS04-T08 should say whether its informed forgery renumbers or is restricted to a device without `seq`.
- **TM-OP-8 Per-device authentication.** Authentication is per device (H-DEP), whereas `attack_signature` of WS04-T08 takes one `auth_deployed`; a mixed deployment (an authenticating cuff, a dispenser that does not) needs a per-device flag, and WS04-T08 test 10 tags the case `expected_undetectable`, which holds for an empty signature (U) and not with authentication deployed.
- **TM-OP-9 INT form (ADR-026).** TX-4, TX-6 and TX-7 use the reference form of INT, decided at the dispense step with a backward window (T02 section 4.7); ADR-026 is proposed and `[DA-CONFERMARE]`; with a forward witness window the INT statements are derived again.
- **TM-OP-10 `auth` masks the witness.** The INT disjunction is satisfied by `auth` whatever the witness reads (TX-7 (a)), so an authenticated release with the robot co-located and nobody present passes INT. Whether INT should also require consistency with a co-located witness is a template question for the freeze (WS01-T08); it is not changed here.
- **TM-OP-11 Row 10 in A.** The analisi row 10 is written without a deployment; here it holds for BLE injection in U and for the physical capabilities, and in A the BLE form leaves `crypto_temporal.authenticated`. WS02-T05 gives the row per deployment, and its expected κ of `injection_informed` (C1 or no alert) holds for U.
- **TM-OP-12 Cross-device evidence and the hierarchy.** Whether `cross_device.pulse_vs_ecg_hr_diff` counts as integrity evidence (step one of ADR-010) or as plausibility context is WS02-T04's; the C2 fault `ecg_noise` raises it.
- **TM-OP-13 Witness quality.** No field says how a witness observation was obtained (modality, confidence, liveness), so an informed spoof cannot be contradicted by a field of the contract; the encoding of identity is WS02-T03 D-6. No field is proposed here.
- **TM-OP-14 Evidence steering.** In U a keyless injector could write the `quality` of the Samples it injects, or deliver a DEVICE_ERROR, NODE_STATE or ROBOT_TASK Event, which feed `signal_quality.*` and `system.*` (WS02-T03 step 4), and steer κ away from C3 (toward C2 or UNDETERMINED) or excuse a cross-device difference; H-EVID keeps those fields as the devices set them and no attack class models the steering. WS04-T08 and WS02-T03 decide whether to add a variant, or to state that those Events are authenticated or produced inside the TCB.
- **TM-OP-15 Verification failures with no attacker, and PENDING instances.** H-AUTHCAUSE excludes a benign verification failure; a payload corruption, a key mismatch or a firmware error would raise `crypto_temporal.authenticated` without an attacker, and the integrity-first hierarchy of ADR-010 would read it as C3. Separately, T05 section 3.5 leaves the follow-up of an elapsed-deadline PENDING instance with a marker (for example INT with `channel_unavailable`, the case of `dispense_forgery` with the robot away) to the attribution layer, while the Attribution contract takes violated records only: whether such an instance raises an alert or a review item is WS02-T04's, and the assumption of the section Attacks that raise no violation depends on it.
