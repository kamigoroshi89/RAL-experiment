# Fault model for C2: system or embodiment failure (device, link, robot)

Document WS02-T01. It is the catalogue of the faults that the benchmark injects on the latent-to-observed path (WS04-T07) and that the ROS 2 simulation injects at run time (WS07-T05), with the signature each fault leaves in trace-schema terms, the `EvidenceVector` fields it raises, the templates it can make violated, the cascades it seeds and the names of its injection parameters. It does not define attribution rules (WS02-T04), the meaning of each evidence field (WS02-T03) or the attacks of class C3 (WS02-T02). Validation in this project is technical and pre-clinical: nothing here is a clinical claim, and no number in this document is a clinical, probabilistic or device value.

The semantics used is the merged formal core: signals, grid, two clocks, reorder buffer and late-sample policy of `docs/formal/01-signals-sampling.md` (called T01 below), the templates, instance semantics and robot obligations of `docs/formal/02-templates.md` (T02), and the three-valued verdicts, stale-open positions and markers of `docs/formal/05-three-valued-semantics.md` (T05). Contracts are those of `docs/plan/02-interfaces.md`; none is changed here, and what the model needs and the contract lacks is recorded as a gap in the section Traceability.

**Reading conventions.**

1. Times are integer milliseconds since the start of the patient-day (ADR-017). A label `hh:mm` converts as `t_ms = (60 hh + mm) * 60000`; in examples the grid step is `T_s` of fixture registry F0 (one minute in the examples), and no unit is written next to a fixture number.
2. FIXTURE-ONLY marks a value chosen for hand arithmetic (fixture registry F0 of `docs/plan/workstreams/WS01-formalization/README.md`); it has no clinical meaning and is not a registry value. "illustrative, unverified" marks a value taken from analisi (ADR-020). Golden cases of `docs/formal/golden/` are written `G-...` (templates) and `V-...` (verdicts); the worked checks of this document are `FX-n` (section Fault effects on templates and cascade seeds); each was computed by hand and re-computed by an independent script that is not committed (outputs in the task file notes).
3. Template ids are the `TemplateId` values `range`, `sched`, `hold`, `count`, `resp`, `sys`, `int`. Verdict states are PENDING, PREDICTED_VIOLATION, VIOLATED, SATISFIED; a PENDING instance whose decision window has a stale-open position carries a marker of T05 section 3.5 (`channel_unavailable:<signal>` or `value_undefined:<signal>`).
4. The contract leaves `Event.payload` open. Payload key names used below (an error code, a commanded marker, a task outcome) describe what the signature must carry and are provisional: WS02-T03 fixes the conventions table.
5. No reference is cited from memory. Where a source would be needed the marker `[ref-needed: WS03]` is written and the analisi section that names the work is given.
6. Alert, violation and attributed cause follow the glossary of `docs/plan/01-conventions.md` section 7: a violation is a violated sub-formula of a template; the attributed cause κ is a value of the alert, not a violation; a device fault is not a violation of the care plan.

## Scope and fault hypothesis

### What C2 is

C2 is a cause class: the attributed cause of a violation of φ_CP, φ_SYS or φ_INT is a failure of a device, of a link or of the robot (system or embodiment failure). It belongs to a cause-based taxonomy over violations of φ_CP ∧ φ_SYS ∧ φ_INT (analisi §4.8), not to a taxonomy induced by the structure of the constraints. The boundary of the class follows ADR-002: patient behaviour that departs from the care plan is C1, and C3 is claimed only as integrity or plausibility violations against the attack classes of WS02-T02, with no claim of detecting tampering in general. The boundary is by cause, not by symptom (section Non-faults and boundaries gives the shared symptoms and the cases that no channel separates).

In the benchmark a true C2 event is an injected event (analisi §5.1): it is in the ground truth whether or not any channel can detect it, and the monitor sees only the corrupted observation. Silent faults (a bias, a plausible stuck value) are kept in the catalogue on purpose, to measure the limit of attribution instead of hiding it (analisi §4.5, §4.8).

### Fault hypothesis

The hypothesis is stated as nine points; each says what a consumer may assume.

1. **Unit of failure.** The unit of failure is the smallest element a fault targets and that has its own identifier in the trace: a device node (the cuff, the ECG device, the dispenser; each has a source identifier, its own clock and its own `seq` where it provides one), a link (the BLE connection of one device to the gateway, the ROS 2 transport behind it, and the time base the device carries, which the monitor sees only as `t_device_ms` against `t_rx_ms`), or a robot node (the processes and resources of the robot: navigation, manipulation, battery; its outputs are the `ROBOT_TASK` Events and the witness channels). A fault targets exactly one unit.
2. **Duration classes.** Three duration classes are used: transient, intermittent and persistent. A transient fault is one window, or one point, after which the unit returns to nominal behaviour. An intermittent fault is a sequence of transient faults of the same subtype on the same unit, separated by nominal behaviour; each member is a separate fault event. A persistent fault has no end within the patient-day: in the benchmark it is a window that runs to the closing step of the day (an injection lies within one patient-day in the plan of WS04-T07, so a longer fault is one injection per day), in the simulation it has no end.
3. **Overlap and simultaneity.** Faults may overlap in time when they have different subtypes or different units; two windows of the same subtype on the same unit may not overlap, and an injector must reject them (the plan of WS04-T07 has a test for it). Simultaneous faults on different units are independent injections: each is specified without reference to the others (its parameters do not depend on whether another injection is realised), and the benchmark injector composes their effects on the same position in a canonical order (robot faults, then device faults, then sequence numbering, then link faults, so that removal comes last; the plan of WS04-T07). A fault that overlaps another is not a cascade: a cascade is a downstream violation of one fault (section Fault effects on templates and cascade seeds).
4. **Single and multiple faults.** Every row of the catalogue states the effect of one fault in an otherwise nominal observed day. For several faults the only statement made is the union of footprints: a position (a signal at a step) emptied or changed by any of them is emptied or changed, and the evidence fields raised are the union. The joint effect on a template is not otherwise claimed, and the root cause of a position emptied by two faults at once is not unique (a declared limit for WS02-T04).
5. **A fault is not a violation.** A fault is a cause. It is not itself a violation of the care plan and not an alert: it may leave the observed trace unchanged (an injection that changes nothing), it may change it without any template instance leaving SATISFIED, and it may violate or leave undecided one or several instances. A violation named after its template is a violation of that template; the attributed cause κ is assigned afterwards by the attribution layer from evidence (ADR-010).
6. **Trusted base.** The ward node (gateway and RSM), the signed care plan and the specification are not units of failure of this catalogue: every statement about verdicts assumes that the monitor runs and receives what the link delivers. The failure of the ward node is the single-point-of-failure question of R3 weakness 2 (ADR-003 adds a watchdog); it is recorded as an open point for the hazard analysis (WS02-T05), not modelled here.
7. **Measurement through the robot visit.** The measurement obligations of SCHED and RESP are executed by the robot (T02 section 5.1), and the benchmark shifts or removes a visit's measurement when the robot is late or fails (the plan of WS04-T07). The robot-fault rows assume this: a robot fault reaches `sched`, `resp`, `hold` and `range` through the measurement the visit carries out, and `int` through the witness channel (for a robot-node target; the device-node targets of `process_kill` and `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` act through the data of that device, section Robot faults and M-DLOSS). A deployment in which the cuff measures on its own schedule, independently of the robot, would not lose measurements to robot faults, and the robot rows on `sched`, `resp` and `hold` would not apply.
8. **Latent-to-observed path.** Faults are applied to the path from the latent trace to the observed trace (analisi §5.1). The latent trace is fault-free, noise-free and attack-free and is used only to compute ground truth; the monitors and the baselines never read `LatentDay` or `GroundTruthEvent`, and the attribution layer reads only the observed trace and the evidence derived from it.
9. **Availability of the robot channels follows the outage.** The availability intervals of `presence` and `intake` come from the robot status and the heartbeat (T01 section 2.5, ADR-003), so a lease lag between the onset of an outage and the moment the channels are marked unavailable is possible. Every statement below that a robot outage leaves INT undecided and not VIOLATED assumes that the channels are marked unavailable no later than the dispense step of the instance (no lag, or a lag shorter than the time from the onset of the outage to that step). The lease value is not given here and the assumption is not confirmed (open point OP-10); FX-9 (e) shows the effect of a longer lag.

What each hypothesis allows is summarised below.

| Hypothesis | A consumer may assume | A consumer may not assume |
|---|---|---|
| single fault | the catalogue row holds as written | that the row holds when another fault touches the same signal |
| multiple, non-overlapping | each fault is an independent injection with its own ground-truth event | any causal link between two faults |
| simultaneous (overlapping, different units or subtypes) | footprints and evidence fields combine by union | a unique root cause for a position emptied twice |
| transient | the unit is nominal before and after the window | that the effect ends with the window (a cascade can outlive it: the counter of `dispenser_bounce` lasts a trailing day, FX-6) |
| intermittent | each window is a separate fault event and a separate injection | that the windows are periodic or correlated |
| persistent | the effect lasts to the closing step of the day, or has no end in the simulation | that the next patient-day inherits the fault (one injection per day) |

Notes on the device rows (claims about templates are derived in the section Fault effects on templates and cascade seeds, with the FX or golden case that checks each).

- **`cuff_detached` and `sys`.** SYS is anchored at a `meas` impulse, and a `MEASUREMENT_MISSED` Event is not a `meas` impulse (T01 section 2.1). The attempt that ends without a value opens no SYS instance, so φ_SYS is not violated in this form; the violations appear in the templates that need a measurement (`sched`, `resp`, `hold`). A cuff that still reported a completed measurement with an unhealthy node or a short inflation would violate φ_SYS (G-SYS-3, G-SYS-2); that is a variant, not the injected form.
- **Silent faults refute "a faulty sensor cannot masquerade as a clinical event".** The statement of the workshop paper is false for them (analisi §4.8): both faults change only the value, and the templates that read the value cannot tell them from the patient (FX-7, FX-8). They are injected to report the rate, not to be detected.
- **`ecg_noise` raises no violation.** No template reads `hr_ecg`, so this fault changes no verdict; it matters only as evidence, and as a confound for the cross-device channel (section Non-faults and boundaries). Where the ECG device reports no quality index, the `Sample.quality` of its samples is `None`: the channel is unavailable, not clean.
- **`dispenser_bounce` and the sequence number.** The extra Event has its own `seq`, so it is counted as a new dispense (T01 section 3.4); a bounce that re-sent an already accepted `(source, seq)` would be treated as a duplicate and not counted, and would raise `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay`, which the hierarchy of ADR-010 can read as integrity evidence (gap FM-GAP-SEQ below). The benchmark assigns sequence numbers after the device faults (plan of WS04-T07), so a bounce is counted.
- **`dispenser_bounce` and the dispense-anchored templates.** The extra `DISPENSE` is an anchor of `count`, `hold`, `resp` and `int` (signal footprint table), so a duplicate instance of each of them exists. The window of the duplicate is that of the real instance displaced by the bin offset of the bounce (M-DUPWIN), so its verdict repeats the real one except near the window edges, where it can differ in both directions (FX-16). `sched`, `range` and `sys` are anchored on other impulses and are unchanged.

## Link and time faults

Units: the BLE connection of one device, the ROS 2 transport, and the time base of a device. Two clocks exist per input (T01 section 4.1): `t_device_ms` is the device timestamp and orders observations, `t_rx_ms` is the receive time in the monitor clock and drives the reorder buffer. The buffer has a declared maximum delay `D` (`reorder_delay_ms`, `MonitorConfig`) and the clock assumption of T01 section 4.4 has a declared bound `skew_max` (`clock_skew_max_ms`, a registry name that is unset until a human sets it). This document gives no value for either.

### Effect on the two clocks and on `seq`, and what the reorder buffer absorbs

The buffer releases the inputs of a step in device-time order and finalises step `k` at the monitor time `t_k + D`. An input with device time `t_dev` and receive time `t_rx` is on time iff `t_rx <= t_{k_up(t_dev)} + D` and its device time does not exceed its receive time by more than `skew_max` (T01 sections 4.2 to 4.4; the test uses the two clocks of the input alone, T05 section 6.1). Monitor behaviour on late samples (counting, the interface of the counters, the loop) is WS05-T04; it is not defined here, and the statements below only say whether an input belongs to the accepted trace of T01.

| id | `t_device_ms` | `t_rx_ms` | `seq` | delivered | absorbed by the reorder buffer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ble_dropout` | unchanged for what is delivered; the window has none | none in the window | a gap in the delivered sequence | no: the data of the window is lost | cannot: loss is not delay |
| `message_drop` | unchanged | none for a dropped message | gaps where `seq` exists | no, for the dropped messages | cannot |
| `link_delay` | unchanged | increased by the delay | unchanged | yes, later | yes iff the total delay `t_rx - t_dev` is at most `D + (t_{k_up(t_dev)} - t_dev)`: always when the delay is at most `D`, never when it is at least `D + T_s`, in between it depends on the phase of `t_dev` inside its step (FX-3) |
| `out_of_order` | unchanged | unequal increases | unchanged, non-monotone in arrival order | yes | yes iff each displaced message meets its own watermark (the same per-input condition; the order of arrival changes nothing, FX-12) |
| `clock_skew` | shifted by the offset | unchanged | unchanged | yes, with a wrong device time | not corrected: within `skew_max` the input is accepted but its position on the grid is wrong; a device ahead by more than the transport delay plus `skew_max` (offset above delay plus `skew_max`, T01 section 4.4 rule 1) is future-stamped (not used, counted); a device behind such that the transport delay plus the offset exceeds `D` plus the phase term of its stamp is late (not used, counted) |

What the buffer can absorb: delay and reordering within the bound `D` (plus the phase term of the step). What it cannot absorb: loss (`ble_dropout`, `message_drop`), delay beyond the bound, and skew beyond the bound; and even within `skew_max` it does not remove the time-base error: window edges are accurate to `T_s` plus up to twice `skew_max` when the two ends of a window come from two devices (T01 section 4.4).

**A delayed sample beyond the bound is a late sample, not a missing one.** It reaches the monitor, is not used (it enters no held value and no counter), is counted per source and is evidence for `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` (T01 section 4.3); a missing sample never arrives and shows, where those exist, as a `seq` gap, a missed deadline or a loss of liveliness. For the verdict they have the same effect (the input is not in the accepted trace, FX-3), and an emitted definitive verdict is never retracted by a late input (V-LATE-1). For the evidence they differ: the late input is counted per source as a late input and raises `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`; the missing one leaves nothing to count and does not raise it.

**Time as an attack surface.** Replay and timestamp manipulation share symptoms with these faults: a late input, a future stamp, a duplicate `seq`. The C3 side belongs to WS02-T02 (the attack classes `replay` and `timestamp_manipulation`); here the boundary is by cause and not by symptom, and the single field `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` does not tell the cause (CG-10). A replayed message that arrives within `D` of the original, or that carries rewritten fresh timestamps, is not caught by timing (T01 section 4.5).

## Robot faults

Units: the robot node (navigation, manipulation, battery, lifecycle) and the processes of device nodes for `process_kill` and `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition`, whose target is the cuff node, the ECG device, the dispenser node or the robot node. These two subtypes are in the robot family because they act on a node process or lifecycle, not because of their target: every claim on a template in their rows and in the effect table states the target it holds for, and a target that is a device node has the effects of a device-side loss of data (a dispenser target loses real `dispense` Events, M-DLOSS). When the robot is not co-located the channels `presence` and `intake` are unavailable (`None`), never 0 (`#signals` rule; T01 section 3.5); a robot fault that keeps the robot out of the room therefore leaves `witness.*` as `None` (the embodied-channel ablation A4 removes the same fields).

Notes on the robot rows.

- **Which robot faults make the robot unavailable as a witness.** `impossible_goal` (robot not in the room), `process_kill` on the robot, `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` on the robot and `battery_depleted` make every `witness.*` field `None`; `nav_delay` does so until the robot arrives; `manipulation_failure` leaves `witness.robot_present` as it was (the robot may be in the room) and must make `witness.intake_observed` `None` for the failed visit (OP-2); `localisation_noise` does so only if navigation fails. The value is `None` because the channel is unavailable, never because no witness was seen (`#evidencevector` rule).
- **The robot is not the monitor.** Under ADR-003 the ward node keeps monitoring the device streams while the robot is out; the robot faults remove the robot's measurements and witness channel, not the continuous monitoring of φ_CP at the ward node. The fallback when the robot is unavailable is escalation to the nurse (ADR-003), which is outside the observed trace.
- **`impossible_goal`, `localisation_noise` and matrix row 5.** Row 5 of the analisi matrix ("robot late to the window") is the closest row: a robot that never arrives and a robot made late by localisation noise present as a robot late to the window.
- **`uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` and publication.** That an inactive node stops publishing is the lifecycle semantics assumed for the simulated device nodes; WS07-T03 confirms it for them (OP-5). If a node kept publishing while inactive, the rows on `sched`, `resp` and `hold` for this subtype would not apply and only the node-state evidence would remain.

## Fault effects on templates and cascade seeds

### Signal footprint of the templates

A fault can change the state of a template instance only through the anchors the instance is created from and the signals it reads in the accepted trace. The footprint of each template, from T02 section 3.1 (the `signals` column) and T05 section 2.3 (the instance bodies), is:

| template | anchor | signals read |
|---|---|---|
| `range` | `meas` Event | `bp` |
| `sched` | schedule tick of the care plan (not a device signal) | `meas` |
| `hold` | `dispense` Event | `meas`, `bp` |
| `count` | `dispense` Event; the day-end marker | `dose_count` (derived from the accepted `dispense` Events), `day_end` |
| `resp` | `dispense` Event | `meas`, `bp`, and the pre-dose value `bp_pre` captured per instance |
| `sys` | `meas` Event | `cuff_health`, `inflation_ms` |
| `int` | `dispense` Event | `presence`, `intake`, `auth` |

Consequence: a fault that changes none of the signals and anchors of a template cannot change any instance of it. This is why `ecg_noise` (it changes `hr_ecg`, which no template reads) violates nothing, why `cuff_silent_bias` and `stuck_at` (they change only `bp`) cannot affect `sched`, `count`, `sys` or `int`, and why a fault of the robot node with the robot as target cannot violate `count` or `sys` (such a fault reaches `sched`, `resp`, `hold` and `range` only through the measurement a visit carries out, hypothesis 7, and `int` through the witness channel, none of which is the anchor or the signal of `count` or `sys`). The same fault types with a device node as target are not covered by that statement: `process_kill` and `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` with the dispenser node as target stop the `dispense` Events that anchor `count`, so they can violate `count` (M-DLOSS, FX-11), and `dispenser_bounce` adds an anchor of `count`, `hold`, `resp` and `int` (M-DUPWIN, FX-6, FX-16). It is not a claim that the converse holds: the table below lists the mechanisms derived, and an effect is claimed only where a mechanism is given.

### Effects on templates

Effect words: `violated` (the instance can be VIOLATED because of the fault), `undecided` (the instance can stay PENDING after its deadline with a marker of T05 section 3.5), `masks` (a violation present on the latent trace is not raised, or a deviation is hidden or displaced), `shifted` (the verdict changes because of a time-base error), `none` (no instance, or no change, where a reader would expect an effect). "Mechanism" names the signal, the position and the verdict state; the check column gives the golden case or the worked check (FX) that reproduces the numbers. All values are FIXTURE-ONLY registry F0 values (registry of the golden files); a statement that an effect "can" happen holds when the stated condition holds in the trace, and it is not a claim about how often it happens.

Mechanism classes referred to in the table.

- **M-REM, measurement removed.** The accepted trace has no `meas` Event for the visit. SCHED: no accepted `meas` in `[k_a, k_d]`, so the guard `no_measurement_after_tick` fails and the instance is VIOLATED at its decision step `k_d` (V-SCHED-3). RESP: no accepted `meas` in the response window, so the guard `obligation_unmet` fails and the instance is VIOLATED at `k_d`, whatever `pre_i` (V-RESP-5; FX-1). HOLD: no `meas` in the look-back window at a dispense step, so the guard `no_recent_measurement` fails and the instance is VIOLATED at the dispense step (G-HOLD-4). RANGE and SYS: no `meas` Event, so no instance is opened.
- **M-LATE, measurement not in the window.** The `meas` Event is accepted but its grid step lies after the window edge (SCHED: after `k_d`, V-SCHED-5b; RESP: after the window end, G-RESP-4), or it is not accepted because it is late (V-LATE-1). The instance is VIOLATED at its decision step with the same guard as in M-REM (for SCHED the interval is graded afterwards by the late reading, V-SCHED-5b).
- **M-VAL, value change on `bp`.** The `meas` Events are present and the `bp` value read by the instance is changed. RANGE: the margin `min(bp - low, high - bp)` changes sign (G-RANGE-1, G-RANGE-2); HOLD: the margin `bp - threshold` changes sign (G-HOLD-2); RESP: the drop `pre_i - bp` changes when the change differs between the pre-dose and the in-window readings (FX-7, FX-8).
- **M-DUP, extra dispense.** An extra accepted `dispense` Event raises `dose_count` by one for the trailing 24 h window (T01 section 5.1); COUNT limit: `dose_max_24h - dose_count` falls by one (V-COUNT-1); the day-end obligation `dose_count - dose_min_day_end` rises by one (G-COUNT-3).
- **M-DUPWIN, extra dispense, shifted window.** An extra accepted `dispense` Event, at a gap shorter than `T_s` after a real one (the analisi nominal interval is below one second, illustrative, unverified), anchors a duplicate instance of every dispense-anchored template (`count` as in M-DUP; `hold`, `resp` and `int` here). Its grid step is that of the real dispense plus a bin offset of 0 or 1 (ceiling rule, T01 section 3.3): the offset is 1 exactly when a grid time lies in `[t, t + gap)`, `t` being the device time of the real dispense, so a real dispense on a grid time always has offset 1. The look-back window of HOLD and of INT and the response window of RESP of the duplicate are those of the real instance displaced by the offset. With offset 0 the duplicate has the real window and the real verdict (a duplicate violation). With offset 1 the two verdicts can differ in both directions: a reading (HOLD, RESP) or a witness (INT) on the step that leaves the window of the duplicate while it stays in that of the real instance gives a real instance SATISFIED and a duplicate VIOLATED, and one on the step that enters the window of the duplicate while it lies after the window of the real instance gives the reverse; the two instances also differ in the margin of INT and in the decision step of RESP. The effect needs a reading or a witness on such an edge step, or a second instance whose obligation is unmet while the first is met; away from the edges the duplicate repeats the verdict of the real instance.
- **M-DLOSS, dispense removed.** A real `dispense` Event is not accepted (a loss on the dispenser link, a dropped message, or a dispenser node that is killed or inactive): the day-end count falls by one (G-COUNT-3). The lost `dispense` anchors no instance, so no HOLD, RESP, INT or COUNT-limit instance is opened for it: a violation that the latent trace has on those instances is not raised (a masked violation, FX-15).
- **M-ROBOT-OUT, robot channels unavailable.** `presence` and `intake` are unavailable at the dispense step and over the INT decision window, and no `auth` Event is accepted: the INT interval is `[-inf, int_window]`, the instance stays PENDING after its deadline with the marker `undecidable: channel_unavailable:presence` (V-INT-1, which keeps `intake` available; when `intake` is unavailable too, as in a robot outage, the marker also names `channel_unavailable:intake` and the interval is the same, T05 section 3.5; FX-9); with an accepted `auth` it is SATISFIED (V-INT-2). This class holds when the channels are marked unavailable no later than the dispense step (hypothesis 9, OP-10): if the marking lags past the dispense step, the held level is read as available and the instance falls under M-PRES (FX-9 (e)).
- **M-PRES, presence evidence lost with the channel available.** The channel is marked available (the robot is co-located, or an outage has not yet been marked, hypothesis 9) but the `presence` Sample that carries the witness is not accepted (lost or late) or does not exist. If an earlier `presence` sample holds the level 0 through the whole decision window, the instance is VIOLATED with the guard `no_witness` (V-INT-3, FX-9); if the earlier sample is more recent than the start of the window, the steps before it are undefined and the instance stays PENDING with the marker `undecidable: value_undefined:presence`, as it does with no sample at or before the dispense (FX-9). The class uses the reference form of INT of T02 section 4.7, decided at the dispense step with a backward window; ADR-026 proposes to keep it and is not confirmed [DA-CONFERMARE] (open point OP-11): with a forward witness window the rows that cite M-PRES, FX-9 (c) to (e) and CAS-5 would have to be derived again.
- **M-STALE, sample lost, event kept.** The `MEASUREMENT` Event is accepted and its `bp` Sample is not (when they travel as separate messages). With no earlier `bp` sample the RANGE position is stale-open: the interval is the hull `[-110, 90]` of F0, PENDING with the marker `undecidable: value_undefined:bp` (T05 section 2.4, Lemma 3 example; FX-10); with an earlier sample the instance reads that older value, a determined number, and decides on it (T05 section 2.1, (H-pair)).
- **M-SKEW, time-base error.** The device time of an input is displaced; the instance reads the input at the displaced grid step (T01 sections 3.3 and 4.4; FX-4, FX-5).

Three limits of the table that the reader should not skip.

- **No row means no derived mechanism, not "cannot".** Where a pair of fault and template has no row, either the footprint excludes it (the template reads none of the signals the fault changes) or this document derives no mechanism. In particular nothing is claimed for `resp`, `hold` or `count` under `clock_skew` and `link_delay` beyond the rows above.
- **`int` is not violated by a robot outage, under hypothesis 9.** Every robot fault that keeps the robot out of the room at the dispense step and over the decision window leaves INT undecided (the marker names the unavailable robot channels), or SATISFIED through an accepted `auth`, and never VIOLATED, because VIOLATED would turn an embodiment failure (C2) into an integrity violation (C3, ADR-002) and would confound ablation A4 (T05 section 3.5). INT is VIOLATED only when the robot channel is available and reads no witness over the decision window (V-INT-3), which a robot outage that covers the decision window cannot produce when the channels are marked unavailable no later than the dispense step (hypothesis 9). If the marking lags past the dispense step and the held `presence` level is 0, the same outage gives a VIOLATED `int` with `no_witness` (FX-9 (e)); that case is excluded by the hypothesis, not by the semantics, and is recorded as OP-10. A link fault on the robot's presence stream can also produce a VIOLATED `int` (rows `message_drop` and `link_delay` on `int`, CAS-5), and that is a hazard for the integrity-first hierarchy rather than a result.
- **Dispense-anchored templates under a lost or an extra `dispense`.** A real `dispense` that is lost opens no instance of `hold`, `resp`, `int` or the COUNT limit, so a latent violation of those instances is not raised (M-DLOSS, FX-15: dispenser link loss, dropped message, killed or inactive dispenser node). An extra `dispense` (`dispenser_bounce`) opens duplicate instances whose verdict repeats the real one except near the window edges (M-DUPWIN, FX-16). Both rows are conditional on the stated target and on the position of the readings and witnesses.

### What the faults do not do

- `ecg_noise` changes no verdict: no template reads `hr_ecg`.
- `cuff_detached`, in the injected form, opens no SYS instance, so φ_SYS is not violated by it.
- `cuff_silent_bias` and `stuck_at` raise no evidence field and leave the `meas` Events, their timing and the sequence numbers unchanged: `sched`, `count`, `sys` and `int` are untouched.
- `ble_dropout` and `message_drop` produce no late input: a lost message is not a late one, and loss does not raise `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`.
- `link_delay` and `out_of_order` within the reorder bound change no verdict and raise no evidence; the buffer absorbs them.
- No robot fault makes `int` VIOLATED while the channels are marked unavailable no later than the dispense step (hypothesis 9): an unavailable channel is not an absent witness.
- `process_kill` of a node gives no transition event from that node, and `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` does not lose liveliness; the two are told apart by liveliness.
- `dispenser_bounce` produces no `INTAKE` (ADR-004) and changes no dispenser content.
- No fault changes the schedule ticks, which come from the care plan and not from a device.
- `localisation_noise` has no template effect except through the navigation outcome.
- No fault reads or alters the latent trace: faults act on the path to the observed trace.

### Cascades

A **cascade seed** is a pair of a fault and a downstream violation for which the semantics gives a dependency path: the downstream instance reads a position that the fault emptied or changed (signal footprint above), and, in the benchmark, the instance would not be violated on the observed trace with that injection removed (the paired design of WS04-T07). Two kinds occur: a fan-out (one emptied position violates several instances that do not cause one another) and a chain (the fault causes an intermediate event that causes the violation). The word does not mean that the downstream violation is a C2 violation: the downstream instance is a violation of its own template (often φ_CP), and the root cause is the fault (analisi §4.9 item 3). This document names the seeds and the evidence that separates a cascade from independent faults; the propagation rules and the root-cause assignment are WS02-T04's and are not defined here.

For every cascade the order of events is given in the fixture timelines (FX-13, FX-14, FX-6) and in the evidence rule: the cascade is told from independent faults by the evidence of the faulted unit lying in the evidence window of every downstream instance (the window and its cut-off are a decision of WS02-T03) and by the downstream instance reading a signal of that unit. Independent faults leave evidence of different units, or no system evidence for one of the violations.

### CAS-1 Cuff data outage: SCHED, RESP, HOLD fan-out

Seeds: `cuff_detached`, `ble_dropout` (cuff link), `process_kill` and `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` (cuff target), `message_drop` (a run of losses), `link_delay` and `clock_skew` beyond the bound. Order of events (FX-13): fault onset 08:00; the readings expected at 08:30 and 09:05 do not arrive; the SCHED instance of tick 09:00 is decided VIOLATED at 09:30; the RESP instance of the 07:00 dispense is decided VIOLATED at 10:00; a HOLD instance is decided VIOLATED at the next dispense if the look-back holds no reading. It is a fan-out: the three violations do not cause one another; each is a consequence of the same emptied position (`meas` events of the cuff). Distinguishing evidence: for `ble_dropout`, `system.node_lifecycle`, and, under the conditions LC and SEQ of the section Fault-to-evidence table, `system.liveliness_ok`, `system.qos_deadline_missed` and `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay`, over the whole interval; for `cuff_detached`, `signal_quality.cuff_error_code` and `system.node_lifecycle`; for `process_kill` (under LC) and `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition`, `system.liveliness_ok` or `system.node_lifecycle`; for `message_drop`, `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` (under SEQ); for `link_delay` and `clock_skew`, `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`. A violation of the same template whose cuff shows none of these is not explained by this fault (it may be another fault, a robot fault or a clinical event). If a day-end measurement-count template existed it would add a fourth violation (analisi §4.6 row 15:00); the library does not have it (CP2, T02 section 4.4), so the day-end link of the workshop example is absent by design.

### CAS-2 Robot unavailability: unmet measurement obligations and an undecided witness

Seeds: `battery_depleted`, `process_kill` and `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` (robot target), `impossible_goal`, `nav_delay` beyond the window, `localisation_noise` that fails navigation, `manipulation_failure` (measurement only). Order of events for a battery fault (FX-14): a battery-low report where the robot reports its battery (precursor); the robot stops at 20:30; the heartbeat is lost and the ward node asserts `system.liveliness_ok` false and escalates to the nurse (ADR-003); the witness channels become unavailable; the dispense at 21:00 has no `auth`, so INT is PENDING with the markers `channel_unavailable:presence` and `channel_unavailable:intake` (not VIOLATED); the tick at 21:30 is decided VIOLATED at 22:00; the RESP instance of the 21:00 dose is decided VIOLATED at 24:00; a HOLD instance at a later dispense is VIOLATED with `no_recent_measurement` if its look-back holds no reading. This is a chain at its start (battery, then a failed or absent visit, then missing positions) and a fan-out after it (SCHED, RESP, and the undecided INT, which read different positions emptied by the same outage). In this cascade the missed witness does not become a violated INT; the "missed INT obligation" is the obligation record `witness_dispense` (T02 section 5.2) left unmet while the template state is PENDING with a marker. Distinguishing evidence: `system.liveliness_ok`, `system.qos_deadline_missed`, `system.nav_task_failed`, `system.manip_task_failed`, `system.battery_low`, with every `witness.*` field `None`. A measurement obligation lies on the path of both units (the robot carries out the visit, the cuff measures): a violation that has robot evidence and no cuff evidence belongs to CAS-2, one that has cuff evidence and no robot evidence belongs to CAS-1, and one that has both has two sufficient causes and no unique root cause (hypothesis 4).

### CAS-3 Dispenser bounce: counter contamination over the trailing day

Seed: `dispenser_bounce`. Order of events (FX-6): the real dispense at 21:00; the bounce half a second later (a fixture gap); the COUNT limit instance of the bounce is VIOLATED at its own step; the extra Event stays in the trailing window, so a real dispense at 07:00 of the next day is VIOLATED on `count` (with the carry-over of state between days, T01 section 5.5), one VIOLATED record per later dispense while the count stays above the limit (T05 section 3.8: one record per instance). It is a chain through the derived counter. It also masks a day-end omission (FX-6). The extra Event additionally anchors duplicate instances of `hold`, `resp` and `int`, whose verdicts repeat the real ones except near the window edges (M-DUPWIN, FX-16): a fan-out from one changed position (the `dispense` impulse) whose members do not cause one another, and, for `int`, a violation of INTEGRITY kind caused by a device fault (OP-12). Distinguishing evidence: none in the current contract (no field for the dispense interval or the content, gap FM-GAP-DISP); the only separation from a patient's double take is evidence that has no field yet (analisi rows 6 and 7), so a cascade of this kind is not separable from a C1 cascade today.

### CAS-4 Silent value faults: repeated value-based violations

Seeds: `cuff_silent_bias`, `stuck_at`. Order of events (FX-7, FX-8): onset; every reading in the window is changed or frozen; each reading opens a RANGE instance with the shifted value, a dispense in the window opens a HOLD instance on the shifted value, the response window reads the shifted drop; the instances are VIOLATED (or SATISFIED where the fault masks a latent violation). It is a fan-out of C1-looking violations with no system evidence. Distinguishing evidence: none; both faults are the declared limit of analisi row 4 (and §4.8): the violated instances reach attribution with no system evidence and with coherent channels, where the hierarchy of ADR-010 gives C1 (its third rule), unless a channel outside the contract separates them; whether they are aggregated into one alert is WS01-T07's.

### CAS-5 Loss of the robot's presence evidence: a link fault that reads as an integrity violation

Seeds: `message_drop` and `link_delay` on the robot's presence stream. Order of events (FX-9): the robot is in the room and sees the patient; the `presence` Sample that carries the witness is dropped or late; the dispense occurs; the held `presence` level is the earlier 0; INT is VIOLATED with `no_witness` (`provenance.template_kind` is INTEGRITY; the reference form of INT of T02 section 4.7, decided at the dispense step with a backward window, ADR-026 not confirmed, OP-11). The hierarchy of ADR-010 puts integrity evidence first, and the evidence that the cause is a link fault is `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` or `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` (and, where raised, `system.qos_deadline_missed`), the first two being the fields that are integrity evidence for replay (gap FM-GAP-SEQ and CG-10). This is a hazard for the attribution rule: a C2 cause can reach a violation of φ_INT, and the document records it so that WS02-T04 and WS02-T05 decide how the evidence is read; it claims nothing about how often it happens.

## Fault-to-evidence table

One row per fault. "Raised" fields are asserted by the fault by construction of its signature; "conditional" fields are raised only when the stated capability or event exists; "becomes `None`" lists the fields whose channel is unavailable because of the fault (never converted to a negative observation, `#evidencevector` rule); the last column gives the `provenance.template_kind` of the violations the fault can cause. `provenance.violated_template` is set by the violated instance, not by the fault. The derivation of every field from the trace and from ROS 2 is WS02-T03's.

**Which columns make the set of paths of a fault.** The set of paths of a fault is the union of the columns "raised" and "conditional". It is the same union as the column "EvidenceVector paths raised" of the catalogue (the two are checked to be equal), and it is the set that the plan of WS04-T07 transcribes into EVIDENCE_SIGNATURES and that WS07-T05 compares with raised_paths ("the set of paths equals the set in its row"). A conditional path belongs to the evidence of a realised injection only when its condition holds in that injection; a consumer that needs one fixed set per subtype takes the union and records the condition. The columns "becomes `None`" and "not raised" are not part of the set: an unavailable field is not raised, and a field that the fault leaves untouched is not raised. For `cuff_silent_bias`, `stuck_at` and `dispenser_bounce` the set is empty.

Conditions used in the table. **LC (lease or deadline condition):** `system.liveliness_ok` is raised only when the silent interval of the unit is longer than the liveliness lease of its stream, and `system.qos_deadline_missed` only when it is longer than the deadline of the stream; a shorter interval raises neither, and the deadline may be undeclared for an event-driven device (WS07-T03 step 7: deadline QoS is declared only for streams with an expected period, and may be null for event-driven devices). Both values are run parameters, and the table assumes that a device stream has its own lease and deadline (gap FM-GAP-LIVE). **SEQ (sequence condition):** `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` is raised by a loss only when the device provides `seq` and a message with a later `seq` is delivered after the loss, because a gap is seen only when the sequence resumes; a persistent loss to the end of the day shows no gap. **D-5:** `system.nav_task_failed` follows a late arrival only when the task layer reports lateness as failure (proposal D-5 of WS02-T03).

## Non-faults and boundaries

### A deliberate lifecycle transition is not a crash

A deliberate lifecycle transition is not a crash. A transition of a node to an inactive state that an operator or the supervisor commanded is a change of state, not a failure: analisi §4.6 (row 15:00) notes that a transition to INACTIVE is a deliberate change of state and that a real crash is detected with liveliness or heartbeat, not with a lifecycle transition. Crash detection uses liveliness and heartbeat (`02-interfaces.md#ros-2-interfaces`). Three cases separate:

| case | `NODE_STATE` Event | liveliness and heartbeat | commanded marker | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| commanded transition (the control `commanded_lifecycle_transition` of WS07-T05) | present | stays asserted | present (announced before the transition) | not a fault: no C2 event in the ground truth |
| `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` | present | stays asserted (the process lives) | absent | a C2 fault: node-state evidence |
| `process_kill` | absent from the node itself (a gateway may generate a link-lost state) | lost | not applicable | a C2 fault: liveliness evidence |

A node-state event alone is therefore not evidence of a fault; the commanded marker must reach the evidence (open point OP-3; the field `system.node_lifecycle` is `Mapping[str, str]`, so a value vocabulary can carry it without a contract change). A missed measurement that follows a commanded deactivation is still a violated SCHED instance; the transition itself is not a C2 fault, and the attributed cause of that violation is not assigned here (WS02-T04).

### Patient behaviour is C1

A patient who refuses or delays a measurement is C1 (ADR-002; the C1 subtype `measurement_refusal`), not C2. The observed symptom is the same as a robot that does not arrive or a cuff that does not complete: no accepted `meas` in the window (M-REM). The cause is separated by evidence about the robot's visit: the navigation and manipulation outcomes (`system.nav_task_failed`, `system.manip_task_failed`) and the witness fields. Without robot evidence (no task outcome, `witness.*` and the robot-derived system fields `None`, no liveliness evidence) the two are not separable and UNDETERMINED is a valid output; with a failed or absent task outcome the system evidence points to C2, and the rule that reads it is WS02-T04's. The confounder group `refusal_vs_robot_missed` of the WS04 README tests the separation. A cuff removed by the patient gives the signature of `cuff_detached` (an error code and a node-state change) and is C1 by cause; the benchmark injects `cuff_detached` as C2 by definition, and the ambiguity in a real deployment is a question for the clinician review. A double take by the patient against `dispenser_bounce` (analisi rows 6 and 7) is the confounder `double_take_vs_bounce`: it is separable only by evidence that has no field yet (dispense interval, content change).

### The C1, C2 and C3 boundary and what no channel separates

- **C1 against C2.** C1 is a clinical or adherence deviation: in the benchmark a violation of φ_CP on the latent trace (analisi §5.1); C2 is an injected failure of a unit. A silent fault that changes only a value (`cuff_silent_bias`, `stuck_at`) produces violations that no channel separates from a clinical deviation: in analisi row 4 there is no independent channel on the pressure and the pulse rate stays coherent, a limit declared and measured on the benchmark. The claim "a faulty sensor cannot masquerade as a clinical event" is false for them (analisi §4.8); the supportable claim is that faults that manifest in node health, in device error codes or in signal quality are attributed to C2, and silent faults with plausible values remain indistinguishable from clinical deviations.
- **C2 against C3.** C3 is an integrity or plausibility violation against a declared attack class (ADR-002), with no claim of detecting tampering in general. The two share symptoms: a `seq` gap (a loss, or injected or replayed messages that break the sequence), a duplicated Event (a bounce that re-sends a `seq` against a replay), a late input (`link_delay`, `clock_skew` behind, against a replayed message), a future stamp (`clock_skew` ahead against timestamp manipulation). The boundary is by cause: a fault is a failure of a unit with no adversary, an attack needs an attacker with a capability of the threat model. Where the evidence is the same, nothing in the trace separates them; authentication of the messages (`crypto_temporal.authenticated`) can separate replayed or injected messages from faults only where it exists, only through the sequence numbers and timestamps it protects, and only for an attacker without the keys (the capability of analisi §4.5: BLE injection and replay without keys); an attacker who holds the keys, or a source without sequence numbers, is outside that statement. Without authentication the separation is partial (analisi row 8: distinguishable with authentication, partial without; row 9: partial). By construction an informed attacker who is coherent on every available channel (analisi row 10) and a consistent silent bias (row 4) are not separable from the phenomena they imitate; they are declared results, not defects of the catalogue.
- **A fault and an attack at once.** The joint attribution is outside this catalogue (WS02-T04, WS02-T05); the benchmark injects them as independent events.
- **Message-level integrity attacks belong to WS02-T02.** Replay, plausible injection, forged dispense and spoofed presence are attack classes of the threat model, including the timestamp and sequence-number attacks that share symptoms with the time faults above.
- **Evidence confound of `ecg_noise`.** A noisy ECG during a real clinical violation can create a cross-device difference that the integrity-first hierarchy could read as incoherence between devices (integrity evidence). `signal_quality.ecg_sqi` is the field that marks the noise; whether the rule uses it first is WS02-T04's decision.

### Other non-faults

- **Measurement noise inside the device error model** is part of every observed day (`DeviceErrorModel.cuff_error_sd_mmhg`), not a fault; a fault is an injected deviation outside it.
- **The robot away from the room** is not a fault. When the robot is not co-located the channels `presence` and `intake` are `None` by design (R3 weakness 2: robot out of range or serving another patient), and the ward node keeps monitoring. A fault is a failure to be present when an obligation required it.
- **A late measurement inside its window** (`nav_delay` smaller than the window) is not a violation and not an alert; the evidence may exist, the violation does not.
- **The ward node, the specification and the care plan** are outside the catalogue (hypothesis 6).

### Contract gaps (not changed here)

The contract of `docs/plan/02-interfaces.md` is not edited. The model needs the following, recorded for the owning tasks (ADR-021 lists several already).

- **CG-5 of T01.** `EventKind` has no member for `auth`, `presence`, `day_end` or `cuff_health`; this document reads them as `Sample` with `signal` equal to the `SignalId` (T01 section 2.2). The link rows on the presence and authentication streams depend on that reading.
- **CG-10 of T01 and ADR-021.** `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible` is one Boolean fed by late arrival, future stamp and replay-related inconsistency, and there is no clock-health field: `link_delay`, `clock_skew` and a replay cannot be told apart by the field. WS02-T03 decides whether the field is split.
- **FM-GAP-SEQ (ADR-021).** `crypto_temporal.seq_gap_or_replay` mixes message loss (a C2 symptom of `ble_dropout` and `message_drop`) and replay (a C3 symptom); under ADR-010 integrity evidence is read first. Affects rows `ble_dropout`, `message_drop`, CAS-5.
- **FM-GAP-DISP (ADR-021).** No field for dispenser content change or the interval between dispense events, although analisi rows 6 and 7 depend on them: `dispenser_bounce` has "no EvidenceVector field yet" for the evidence that would separate it from a double take.
- **FM-GAP-FLAT.** No field for a run of identical values (the evidence that would flag a `stuck_at` whose value is implausible for the signal); the plausible stuck value stays a declared limit.
- **FM-GAP-NODE.** `Event.payload` has no keys fixed for `NODE_STATE` (state, commanded marker), `DEVICE_ERROR` (error code), `ROBOT_TASK` (outcome, kind of task) or `DISPENSE` (content); the names used here are descriptions.
- **FM-GAP-LIVE.** `system.liveliness_ok`, `system.qos_deadline_missed`, `system.battery_low` and `system.diagnostics_ok` are scalars that do not say which node they refer to; only `system.node_lifecycle` is per node. The contract names the liveliness and deadline QoS on `/ralmon/samples` and `/ralmon/heartbeat`; the rows on device targets assume that the stream of one device has its own liveliness lease and deadline (as the per-device topics of the simulation have), which WS05-T09 and WS07-T03 confirm.

**Evidence with no EvidenceVector field yet, listed for WS02-T03:** the dispense interval and the content change (FM-GAP-DISP); a run of identical values (FM-GAP-FLAT); clock health (CG-10); the distinction between a sequence gap and a replay inside one field (FM-GAP-SEQ); the commanded marker of a node transition, which a value vocabulary of `system.node_lifecycle` can carry without a contract change (OP-3).

### Open points

- **OP-1 Witness perception failure is not catalogued.** A false negative of the robot's perception (the patient is present, the channel reads 0) is a robot fault with no subtype here; it would make INT VIOLATED and the hierarchy would read an integrity violation (analogous to CAS-5). Candidate for a new subtype and for WS02-T02 (presence is not identity, R3 weakness 5). No value or rate is proposed.
- **OP-2 Encoding of a failed visit on the witness channel.** After `manipulation_failure` the robot may be in the room but cannot witness: `witness.intake_observed` must be `None`, not a denial. WS02-T03 fixes the convention (the failed-visit case is not listed among its decisions; it is related to its decision D-6, the dependency among the witness fields); this document requires it.
- **OP-3 Commanded marker.** Whether the commanded flag lives in the value vocabulary of `system.node_lifecycle` or elsewhere is WS02-T03 (its decision D-4).
- **OP-4 Diagnostics source.** No catalogued fault is attributed to `system.diagnostics_ok`; whether the simulated nodes publish ROS 2 diagnostics is WS07-T03. A device battery fault is not catalogued either: its trace effect is that of `ble_dropout` or `process_kill` of the device, and the meaning of the single `system.battery_low` (device or robot) is WS02-T03 (its decision D-2).
- **OP-5 Publication of an inactive node.** The rows on `uncommanded_lifecycle_transition` assume that an inactive node does not publish; WS07-T03 confirms or corrects it.
- **OP-6 Injectors for the modelled-only faults.** `link_delay`, `out_of_order`, `clock_skew` and `battery_depleted` have no injector in WS04-T07 or WS07-T05, and `manipulation_failure` has none in the simulation; their effects are checked here by fixture examples only. The task that should adopt them (an extension of WS07-T05, or hand-built traces in the monitor tests of WS05-T04) is to be decided by the plan lead. The jitter of a delay and the drift of a clock are not parametrised here.
- **OP-7 Separate or joint drops.** Whether the gateway drops the `Sample` and the `Event` of one measurement together or independently (the pair of T05 section 2.1, (H-pair)) is a gateway and transport property; rows `message_drop` on `range` (M-STALE) apply only if they can be dropped independently.
- **OP-8 Propagation rules.** The root-cause assignment and the cascade links of the alert (`root_cause_of`, `caused_by`) are WS02-T04 and WS01-T07; this document gives only the seeds, the order of events and the separating evidence. If a day-end measurement-count template were adopted by ADR (CP2), CAS-1 would gain the day-end link of analisi row 15:00.
- **OP-9 The ward node.** Its failure is the open single-point-of-failure question (hypothesis 6, ADR-003 watchdog): for the hazard analysis of WS02-T05.
- **OP-10 Detection lag of a robot outage (hypothesis 9).** The availability intervals of `presence` and `intake` come from the robot status and the heartbeat (T01 section 2.5), so they can start after the outage does. The rows that leave INT undecided under a robot outage hold when the channels are marked unavailable no later than the dispense step; with a longer lag and a held `presence` level of 0 the instance is VIOLATED with `no_witness` (FX-9 (e)), a C2 cause read as an integrity violation. The heartbeat lease is not given a value here; WS05 and WS07-T03 decide how the availability mask is produced and with which lag, and WS02-T04 decides how a VIOLATED `int` with robot evidence is read.
- **OP-11 INT form (ADR-026).** M-PRES, FX-9, FX-16 (c) and CAS-5 use the reference form of INT, decided at the dispense step with a backward window (T02 section 4.7, TG-8). ADR-026 proposes to keep it and is `[DA-CONFERMARE]`; with a forward witness window these statements have to be derived again.
- **OP-12 A device fault that reaches an integrity violation.** `dispenser_bounce` can make a duplicate `int` instance VIOLATED (`provenance.template_kind` INTEGRITY) where the real instance is SATISFIED, when a witness lies on the window edge (FX-16 (c)), and the integrity-first hierarchy of ADR-010 can read it as integrity evidence. The case is analogous to CAS-5; how it is read is WS02-T04's and WS02-T05's, and the document does not say how often it happens.
- **OP-13 Parameter names and the shape of the magnitude.** The sheet uses the mapping form of WS07-T05; WS04-T07 has a scalar magnitude, a magnitude unit and a params field and none of `gap_ms`, `bias_mmhg`, `noise_level`, `delay_ms`, `seed`, `target` (comparison table of the section Injection parameter sheet). Only `onset_ms` and `duration_ms` match. A choice between adapting the plan of WS04-T07 to the sheet and adapting the sheet is for the plan lead; neither task file is edited here. WS07-T05 does not name the schedule keys of the duration and of the target.
- **OP-14 `out_of_order` within the bound and `crypto_temporal.timestamp_implausible`.** Step 4 of WS02-T03 (task WS02-T03, line 56) derives the field from `t_device_ms` against `t_rx_ms` and also from non-monotonic device time. This document says that `out_of_order` within the reorder bound raises no evidence (the effect rows and the section What the faults do not do), because the buffer restores device-time order before the accepted trace (FX-12). If the field is computed on arrival order before the buffer, `out_of_order` within the bound raises it, and the evidence rows of `out_of_order` and `link_delay` would change. WS02-T03 and WS05 settle it; not edited here.
- **OP-15 Duration of `nav_delay`.** The sheet gives `nav_delay` a `duration_ms` for the simulation schedule; WS04-T07 injects it per tick (test 10, line 50) and gives per-tick faults end_ms `None` (test 13, line 53). The two plans are compatible only if the benchmark ignores the duration; not decided here.
- **OP-16 `cuff_detached` and the node-state event.** The catalogue lists a `NODE_STATE` Event of the cuff node for `cuff_detached` (analisi row 3: change of node state) and the evidence table raises `system.node_lifecycle`; test 5 of WS04-T07 (line 45) lists `MEASUREMENT_MISSED`, `DEVICE_ERROR` with an error code, `cuff_health` and `inflation_ms`, and no `NODE_STATE` Event. Either the benchmark adds the event or the set of paths of `cuff_detached` loses `system.node_lifecycle`; not decided here.
